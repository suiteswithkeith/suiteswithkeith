The Best Dolomites Hotels, By Why You’re Going

I stayed at three and toured two in October 2026. There isn’t one “best” hotel here — there’s the right one for your trip.

5 Luxury Hotels  ·  October 2026 Stays & Site Visits

Tell me where you’re going The hotels

In the Dolomites, the question isn’t which hotel is “best.” It’s why you’re going.

In October 2026 I stayed at Forestis, Gardena Grödnerhof and COMO Alpina, and toured Aman Rosa Alpina and Miramonti. Each serves a different purpose, and all of them range from very solid to outstanding in their own right. Coming for the spa and the views? For hiking from your door? For skiing? For restaurants within a short walk? The answer changes the hotel.

Gardena Grödnerhof was my personal favorite, so it’s first. The rest aren’t ranked against each other — they’re built for different trips, and I’ve noted who each one suits.

Booking through me costs the same as booking direct, and my clients typically receive preferred-partner perks on top: a space-available upgrade, daily breakfast and a hotel credit. I also handle what makes the Dolomites work: trains and private drivers, lift times, mountain guides and helicopters.

01

Gardena Grödnerhof

My Favorite Hotel In The Dolomites

Perfect For · Couples, Families, Hikers, Foodies

Right in Ortisei, a super cute town with great restaurants and lifts to two completely different hiking areas: Seceda for the dramatic peaks of Val Gardena, and the gondola to the meadows of the Alpe di Siusi. Add an incredible spa, a fantastic gym, excellent service and a beautiful renovation of most of its roughly 80 rooms, and I was blown away.

Book a Superior Deluxe or above. Those categories are beautifully renovated. The Superior and Comfort rooms keep the original charm and are spacious, but they’re not why you come.

Skip July and August if you want quiet. I visited in October; in peak summer the crowds in Ortisei, and on the trails, would be frustrating.

Great skiing, not ski-in, ski-out. The slopes are all around you, but you’ll walk or shuttle to the lifts.

The most well-rounded hotel in the Dolomites, for couples and families alike, in the best town for restaurants and varied hiking.

02

Forestis

The Best Wellness Retreat In The Dolomites

Perfect For · Honeymoons, Couples, Wellness, Total Disconnection

High above Bressanone with the best view of the Odle of any luxury hotel in the region, Forestis is about disconnecting: the spa, the scenery, the food and the quiet. It won my categories for wellness, privacy, views, the indoor pool, dining and service.

Ask for a high floor in the right tower. The best views are from the fourth or fifth floor up, in the tower closest to the lodge or the middle tower. Only a request secures it.

Not a hiking or skiing base. Come for the spa and the quiet; the region’s best trails and slopes are a drive away.

Adults 14 and over. Families should look at the five-bedroom Forestis Villa.

For peace and quiet, wellness, great food and awe-inspiring views, there’s nowhere better in the Dolomites.

03

Aman Rosa Alpina

The Best Luxury Hotel In The Dolomites

Perfect For · Families, Couples, Skiers

A beautiful restoration of the former Rosa Alpina in San Cassiano, in a fantastic spot for hiking and skiing, and as good for families as for couples. The design is classic Aman, all wood and marble, and the star is the suites, with views of the forest, the village and the mountains above.

Intimate views, not dramatic ones. For big panoramas, Forestis, COMO Alpina and Miramonti win.

Great for skiing, not ski-in, ski-out. There’s a ski room, and the lifts are about two minutes away by shuttle.

Winter minimums. The hotel mentioned a seven-night minimum in winter that searches don’t show. I confirm it before you plan around it.

The most luxurious stay in the region. Book a suite, and come in winter or summer when San Cassiano is buzzing.

04

COMO Alpina Dolomites

The Only True Ski-In, Ski-Out

Perfect For · Skiers, Hikers, Wellness

Right on the Alpe di Siusi, the rolling meadows ringed by peaks just over the mountain from Ortisei. It’s a protected area — cars need a permit — and you can walk out of your room onto the trails or the slopes. Not quite the hotel Forestis, Gardena or Aman is, but the price reflects that, and the spa and pool hold their own against all of them.

Mind the chairlift. A chairlift runs right outside the Mountain View rooms, which makes them a little loud and less private, especially in the shoulder seasons.

Big rooms. Spacious, with decks, large bathrooms and tubs.

Good service, with the odd awkward moment. The formal restaurant was a highlight.

My first choice for a ski trip, and tied with Gardena Grödnerhof for summer.

05

Miramonti

The Iconic Pool Above Merano

Perfect For · Couples, Design Lovers, Pool People

A unique, quirky hotel close to Merano, with an iconic pool and beautiful views. Every room is designed differently. Merano isn’t really “in” the Dolomites like the others, and it’s not at their level as a luxury hotel experience, but for the right traveler it’s a great stay.

The best outdoor pool I saw. It’s the reason people come.

Character over polish. Choose it for personality and views, not white-glove service.

A great hotel for the right traveler, and an easy add-on at either end of a Dolomites trip.

Two hotels, one trip

The pairings I’d book

Splitting a trip between two hotels gives you two very different stays, and the drives between them are about an hour.

01
Gardena Grödnerhof + Forestis. Two or three nights in Ortisei for hiking and restaurants, then two or three at Forestis to slow down with the spa and the view. Town energy, then complete quiet.
02
COMO Alpina + Gardena Grödnerhof. Two or three nights on the Alpe di Siusi meadows and two or three in Ortisei, just over the mountain. The meadows, then the peaks of Val Gardena, with a short transfer between them.
03
Add Miramonti at either end. Merano is an easy start or finish, especially if you’re coming from or heading toward Lake Como or Switzerland.
Know before you go

Weather, connections & transfers

The Dolomites take more planning than most of Italy. Here’s what I tell clients before they book.

01
Summer is hiking season, and peak crowds. From late June to mid-September every lift is running and the days are long, but Ortisei, Seceda and the Alpe di Siusi are at their busiest. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in July and August, so start hikes early.
02
September and early October are my favorite. Quieter trails, crisp clear days and, by mid-October, golden larches. The trade-off: lifts start closing in October, and villages like San Cassiano go quiet.
03
Watch the closing dates. Many hotels and lifts close between seasons, roughly April to May and from late October or November into December. Check before you set dates; I do this for every trip.
04
Winter is for skiing. From December to March the Dolomiti Superski network links the valleys. COMO Alpina is the only true ski-in, ski-out hotel here; Aman Rosa Alpina is a two-minute shuttle from the lifts, and Gardena Grödnerhof is close to them in Ortisei.
05
Know the sauna rules before you go. The main sauna areas are mixed, men and women together. You’ll walk around the spa in a towel or robe, but swimsuits and wraps come off in the saunas and steam rooms. Your body isn’t supposed to touch the wood, so you sit or lie on your towel in the saunas, and on a small towel in the steam rooms. The culture here is very German and Austrian, and it applies at every hotel on this list. The pools are swimsuit areas.
06
Pack layers, whatever the month. Mountain weather changes fast, and it can be much colder at the top of a lift than in the valley.
07
Flying in. Innsbruck is the closest major airport, about two hours from Ortisei or Forestis. Verona is about two and a half hours, and Venice, Munich and Milan are about three to four, depending on the hotel and traffic.
08
By train. The main line from Verona runs up through Bolzano and Bressanone to Innsbruck and Munich. Get off at Bolzano for Ortisei, the Alpe di Siusi or Merano, or at Bressanone for Forestis, and finish by private car.
09
Between the hotels. Most pairings are about an hour by car: Ortisei to Forestis, Ortisei to San Cassiano over the Gardena Pass, and Ortisei to Merano. COMO Alpina is just over the mountain from Ortisei.
10
Private drivers beat self-driving. Mountain passes are narrow and winding, parking in Ortisei is tight in summer, and the road up the Alpe di Siusi is restricted to permit holders. A private driver takes all of that off your plate, and I arrange them.
11
Book the busy lifts and the guides ahead. The Seceda lift needs a time-slotted ticket bought in advance; the Col Raiser lift from Santa Cristina, about 10 minutes from Ortisei, reaches the same area with some planning. I’d always choose a private guide over going with the crowds.
12
Go by helicopter, if the budget allows. A helicopter transfer from Lake Como, Venice or Milan saves hours, and a flight up to the high ground followed by a guided hike is the most memorable day you can have here. I arrange both.
