Lake Como · The pairing

Two honeymoons,
one lake

Two nights in an 18th-century villa. A ten-minute private boat across the water. Two nights in the most modern hotel on the lake. Same couple, two completely different stays.

Plan this for us See the crossing

Most people pick one Lake Como hotel and spend the whole trip there. The better move is to pick two that have nothing in common.

Passalacqua is the villa you’ve seen everywhere: an 18th-century estate in Moltrasio, restored room by room, with silk on the walls, gilded headboards and gardens stepping down to the water. Il Sereno sits almost directly across the lake in Torno, and it’s the opposite in every way: glass, walnut and stone, every room facing the lake, and a heated pool just above the water. It’s quiet, private and built for couples.

Two hotels that look nothing alike, but the same kind of person loves both. If you’re planning a honeymoon or a milestone trip, that’s probably you. The part that ties it together is the ten-minute private boat between them, which is also the best part of the trip.

Stay one · Moltrasio

Passalacqua

The villa. Like staying at an Italian family’s estate that happens to have 24 rooms.

Built in 1787 and opened as a hotel in 2022, it was named the world’s best hotel within a year. Inside it’s layered and romantic: frescoed ceilings, silk-covered walls, antiques you’re allowed to sit on. Outside, the gardens fall in terraces toward the lake.

It runs like a private home. Only 24 rooms, and the staff learn your name on day one.

The gardens are the hotel. Terraces, cypress, a pool lined with striped umbrellas, and a clay tennis court above the water.

Book the Junior Suite Al Lago. It opens toward the water, and it’s the room I show couples first.

The part everyone asks about

Ten minutes across the water

No car, no switchbacks, no hour on the lake road. You leave one hotel from the water and arrive at the next one the same way.

01Slow morning. Breakfast in the villa, one more swim. There’s no ferry to catch.
02Down to the water. Your boat and captain are waiting, and your bags come with you.
03Across the lake. About ten minutes, straight across, with the whole lake opening up around you.
04Step off at Il Sereno. The hotel is on the water, so the dock is the front door.

My favorite trick: don’t take the short way. Have the boat go north first, past Villa del Balbianello and Bellagio, then drop you at Il Sereno. Your transfer becomes your boat day, and the check-out day turns into the best afternoon of the trip.

Stay two · Torno

Il Sereno

The modern one. Private, quiet and built for couples, with every room looking at the lake.

I’ve stayed here, and I couldn’t find a single fault. Patricia Urquiola’s design has a Japanese-minimalist, warm-industrial feel, all glass, walnut and stone. There’s a listening suite that feels like a great hi-fi bar, and a Michelin-starred restaurant on the water. Torno is a tiny village, and you can only get into the hotel if you’re staying or you come by boat, so there are almost no day visitors. After two nights of gilt and frescoes, it feels like a completely different trip.

The pool is on land, and heated. A lot of Como pools float on docks and rock with every passing boat. This one doesn’t, and the poolside service is excellent.

The hot tub in the old boathouse. Book it just for the two of you, by the hour.

Every room sees the lake. Entry rooms get a partial view, some through the trees. The Grand Suite is where it gets special, and it starts at about what an entry room costs at Passalacqua.

Their own fleet of Rivas. The dock is the front door, and a boat to dinner across the lake is easy to arrange.

The sheets. The softest I’ve ever slept on. I’m still thinking about them.

Next time I’m going back for at least two nights and pairing it with Passalacqua. That’s this whole page.

Who it’s for: couples who want the famous villa and also want to feel like they went somewhere new halfway through. You get two honeymoons in one trip, and only one travel day.

How it runs

Four nights, two hotels, one boat

The two-and-two version. For a honeymoon, I’d stretch it to three and three, which adds one full do-nothing day at each hotel.

Day 1
Land in Milan. A car takes you to Moltrasio, about an hour. Gardens, an aperitivo on the terrace, dinner at the villa.
Day 2
Passalacqua, all day. The pool, the clay court, nowhere to be.
Day 3
Late breakfast, then down to the water. Ten minutes across the lake and you’re checking in at Il Sereno.
Day 4
Il Sereno, all day. The pool and the boathouse hot tub, then one of the hotel’s Rivas to dinner across the lake if you want it.
Day 5
Car to Milan for your flight home.

Staying farther up the lake?

Arrive by boat anyway

If your hotel is up in the middle of the lake, the boat is the better way in.

Grand Hotel Tremezzo, Villa Serbelloni in Bellagio and the Lake Como EDITION are all a long way up the lake from the town of Como. The road there is narrow and winding, and it takes 45 minutes to an hour. That’s a rough last stretch after an overnight flight.

So I split it in two. Your car from Milan, or wherever you’re coming from, drops you at the water in Como. A private boat is waiting, your bags go on board, and about 30 minutes later you step off at your hotel. On a honeymoon, that’s a much better first hour on Lake Como than the lake road.

Before you ask

The questions I get

How long is the boat between Passalacqua and Il Sereno?

About ten minutes. Passalacqua sits in Moltrasio on the west shore and Il Sereno in Torno on the east shore, almost directly across the water from each other. It is a private boat, booked for your time, with your bags on board.

How many nights should we do at each?

Two and two is the minimum that still feels like two separate stays. Three and three is the version I’d book for a honeymoon, because it gives each hotel a full day where you don’t go anywhere at all.

Which hotel should come first?

I usually start at Passalacqua. It is the harder confirmation, so we lock those dates first and build Il Sereno around them, and the villa is the better place to land and slow down after a long flight. The reverse works too if the dates fall that way.

We&rsquo;re staying in Bellagio or Tremezzo. Can we arrive by boat?

Yes, and I’d book it that way. A car brings you from Milan to the town of Como, and a private boat takes you up the lake from there. It’s about 30 minutes on the water, compared with 45 minutes to an hour on the lake road, and it works for Grand Hotel Tremezzo, Villa Serbelloni and the EDITION.

Is Il Sereno good for families?

Not really. It’s built for couples who want privacy, lake views and design. Parents with older teenagers could make it work if the kids are happy by the pool, but for a family trip I’d point you somewhere else on the lake.

Is Torno too quiet?

Torno is a tiny village, and that’s the point: nobody wanders into the hotel. If you want an evening out, Bellagio, Como and the restaurants across the lake are a short boat ride away, and I can line those boats up for you.

When can we do this?

Both hotels are seasonal and close for winter. Roughly April through October is the window, and May, June and September are the months I push hardest.
