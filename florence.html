In the city or in the hills above it: the four Florence hotels I recommend, who each one is for, and how to pair the city with the Tuscan countryside.

Plan my Florence stay

The first Florence decision isn’t which hotel. It’s whether you want to sleep in the city or above it.

Stay in the center and you walk out the door into Florence, with the Duomo, the Uffizi and the Oltrarno all on foot. Stay in the hills and you trade that for quiet, gardens and a view of the whole city, with a walk or a shuttle into town.

I’ve stayed at Collegio alla Querce and toured Four Seasons Firenze and Villa San Michele in person, and I’m a preferred partner of Four Seasons, Auberge and Belmond, so my clients get an upgrade when available, daily breakfast and a hotel credit, at the same rate as booking direct.

Where I’d stay in Florence

In the city · On my Italy collection

Hotel Lungarno

Riverside boutique (Lungarno Collection)

The Florence hotel for travelers who came to be in Florence. Cross the bridge and you’re at the Uffizi in minutes, while the Oltrarno side keeps you among the artisans and wine bars where Florentines actually live.

Right for
First-time Florence visitors, couples, walkers
Room I’d book
Book the river view specifically.The Arno-facing rooms are the entire point, so I confirm the room category as well as the hotel.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Boutique scale, boutique facilities. No resort pool or garden here. For that, it's the Four Seasons Firenze. The city is the amenity.

The Oltrarno side is the right side. Quieter at night, better at aperitivo hour, and still two minutes from the bridge.

Transfers and drivers, arranged. I arrange private transfers from Florence’s airport or Santa Maria Novella station, and drivers for days in Chianti, Siena or the Val d’Orcia, so nobody has to navigate Tuscan back roads or park in a hill town.

The verdict

Ponte Vecchio views, steps from everything. The Florence hotel for travelers who came to be in Florence.

In the city · Toured in person

Four Seasons Hotel Firenze

Palazzo resort (Four Seasons)

A Renaissance palazzo set in sprawling private gardens that feel impossible this close to the Duomo, with Michelin-starred dining on site and refined, formal service. My pick for families and for anyone staying long enough to want a resort in the city.

Right for
Families, longer Florence stays, garden lovers

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

It's a walk to the core sights. About ten minutes to the Duomo. A gentle stroll for most, but worth noting if you want to be on top of the postcard.

The palazzo rooms vary wonderfully. Frescoes here, garden views there. Tell me what matters and I'll request the right one.

Families, this is your Florence hotel. The garden and pool make it the city's easiest luxury stay with kids, which is rare and worth planning around.

The verdict

Resort-like calm inside the city center, and the only Florence hotel where the garden competes with the art.

In the hills · Stayed

Collegio alla Querce

Modern hillside retreat (Auberge)

A brighter, more modern take on Florence, about a 30-minute walk from the center, with an impressive art collection and sweeping views from almost every room. I’ve slept here, so I can tell you which rooms and views to ask for.

Right for
Return visitors, couples, families who want space to breathe

In the hills · Toured in person

Belmond Villa San Michele

Historic hillside retreat (Belmond)

A renovated 15th-century former monastery built into the Fiesole hillside, with terraces stepping down the slope and a private shuttle into town. Spring through fall, when the terraces and pool are the point, is when it sings.

Right for
Couples, view seekers, second-time Florence visitors

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

You're up above the city. The shuttle makes it easy, but if you want to walk out into the streets, look at Hotel Lungarno (my riverside pick, steps from everything).

Privacy varies by room. There's less privacy than you'd expect at this level. I'll make sure you're in a private room. The most secluded garden suite overlooks the parking lot, but only the chapel room is more private.

Lots of stairs up to the pool. The pool is a real climb from the rooms. Worth knowing if anyone in your group has mobility concerns.

The verdict

The best views in Florence, from a renovated 15th-century monastery. The city's most romantic address isn't in the city at all.

Know before you go

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

01
Don’t drive into the center. The historic center is a restricted traffic zone, and cameras fine unregistered cars. Arrive by private transfer or train, and pick up a car (or a driver) only when you leave for the countryside. I arrange both.
02
Decide city or hills first. In the center you walk everywhere; in the hills you trade that for quiet and a view, with a walk (Collegio) or a hotel shuttle (Villa San Michele) into town.
03
Two or three nights is the sweet spot. Enough for the Duomo, the Uffizi and a long lunch in the Oltrarno without rushing. Then head into Tuscany, where the trip slows down.
04
Book the big museums ahead. The Uffizi and the Accademia sell out in high season. Tell me your dates and I’ll line up timed entries or a private guide with the rest of the trip.
Make it one trip

Florence, then the countryside.

Most of my Italy clients do two or three nights in Florence, then move out into Tuscany (a restored borgo, a castle or a wine estate) for the slow part of the trip.
