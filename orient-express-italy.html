Before you board

The La Dolce Vita lounge

La Dolce Vita has its own private terminal at Rome Ostiense, and it sets the tone for everything after.

You check in at the front desk, where your “boarding pass” and everything for the journey is waiting in a numbered pigeonhole behind the counter. Then it’s free-flowing Champagne and snacks in a lounge designed like the train itself: lacquered gold ceilings, travertine, velvet armchairs and a mosaic floor.

The lounge

The details

The buzz in the lounge before the train was so much fun. Everyone was excited, almost like Christmas morning, and I’ve never seen that in this industry. Arrive early and enjoy it. It’s part of the trip.

The hotels at either end

La Minerva and Venice

Rome · stayed October 2026

Orient Express La Minerva

The first Orient Express hotel anywhere, in a seventeenth-century palazzo on Piazza della Minerva, steps from the Pantheon. Hugo Toro designed it as a love letter to the Grand Tour. 93 rooms including 36 suites, a seventh-floor rooftop restaurant looking over the Pantheon, and Two MICHELIN Keys.

Orient Express Venice

Palazzo Donà Giovannelli, a fifteenth-century palace on a quiet canal corner in Cannaregio, after an eight-year restoration. It is almost overwhelming how beautiful it is: dark wood, some seventy kinds of marble, restored ceilings and Murano chandeliers.

Before you ask

The questions I get

How much does La Dolce Vita Orient Express cost?

Fares are dynamic and change with the season and the journey. As a guide, Deluxe Cabins start around €3,500 per person per night, and suites run higher. I quote exact fares for your dates, along with any current offers.

Can I take the train from Rome to Venice?

Yes. For 2027 there is a Rome to Venice journey via Bolgheri, one way. It is the one I recommend for pairing Orient Express La Minerva in Rome with Orient Express Venice.

How many cabins are on the train?

Twelve Deluxe Cabins, eighteen Suites and one La Dolce Vita Suite, for up to 62 guests. It is small, so the best cabins on popular dates go first.

Is La Dolce Vita Orient Express worth it?

Yes. If you're thinking about doing it, the answer is do it. It is a unique experience, and the buzz in the lounge before boarding is unlike anything else I have seen in travel.

Are the cabins small?

Yes, and that is worth knowing going in. Every cabin has its own bathroom, but even the suites are compact and remind you that you are on a train. It shouldn't stop you from booking. It just sets your expectations correctly.

What should I pack?

A carry-on. For one to three nights on board you don't need much, and the cabins aren't built for big suitcases.

How many people are on the train?

Usually around 40 to 60 guests, with room for up to 62. Expect to be social and have fun.

What is the La Dolce Vita lounge like?

It is a private terminal for the train at Rome Ostiense. You check in at the front desk and collect your boarding pass, then enjoy free-flowing Champagne and snacks before boarding. Arrive early; the atmosphere is part of the experience.

Can we charter the whole train privately?

Yes, on request. The full train can be privatized for up to 62 guests, subject to availability and operational feasibility, with routes built around your group and bespoke menus. Smaller groups can privatize selected carriages.

Who does the food on the train?

The culinary program is led by Heinz Beck, the three-Michelin-star chef behind La Pergola in Rome.

Where does the train depart from?

Journeys start in Rome, from the La Dolce Vita lounge at Ostiense station, and some start in Palermo. That is why I put Rome and La Minerva at the start of the trip.

Can you add the Orient Express yacht?

Yes. Orient Express Corinthian sails the Mediterranean in summer, including the Italian Riviera and the Adriatic, and the Caribbean in winter. Tell me your dates and I will tell you which sailings line up with the train.
