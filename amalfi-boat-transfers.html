There is no airport on the Amalfi Coast, and every road in is a cliffside switchback.

After an overnight flight, an hour and a half of hairpin turns is a hard way to start a trip. The sea is the other way in. Your driver meets you at arrivals in Naples, takes you to the port, and a private boat runs you down the coast to your hotel. You skip the road entirely, and you see the coast the way it was meant to be seen, from the water, with Capri on one side and the cliffs on the other.

Each transfer is arranged individually. Boat, captain, route and timing depend on your flight, your hotel and the sea that day. One thing to know up front: I am not a boat-ticket desk. The transfer comes as part of the hotel work, for trips whose hotels I am booking, or as an add-on to a hotel booking you have already made and hand over to me to manage.

No. 01

Why go by boat

The arrival, upgraded

Best for · long-haul arrivals, honeymoons, anyone who gets carsick

The road is beautiful, and it is also a lot of curves after a long flight. By boat, the first hour of your trip is the coast itself: Vesuvius behind you, Capri off the bow, and the villages stacked up the cliffs as you come around the headland.

No road at all. The cliff road is narrow and busy in season, with buses and scooters on every bend. The boat skips all of it.

It can be the first afternoon of the trip. Stop at Capri for lunch, or for a swim at a cove you can only reach from the water, and arrive at your hotel in time for the sunset.

Someone is with you the whole way. Your driver is waiting at arrivals with your name on a sign, the bags go from car to boat to hotel, and you never stand in a ferry line.

If you can’t do winding roads, this is the answer. And even if you can, it is the nicer way in.

No. 02

The routes that work

Pick the port by the hotel

Rule of thumb · Naples for Positano, Salerno for Amalfi

Where you are staying decides where the boat leaves from. The coast runs east to west, and the right port puts you on the shortest stretch of water to your hotel.

Naples to Positano. The classic. Your driver takes you from Naples airport to the port, and the boat runs across the bay and around the Sorrento peninsula to the Positano hotels: Il San Pietro, Villa Treville and Le Sirenuse.

Salerno to Amalfi. For the Amalfi side, Santa Caterina, Miramalfi and Borgo Santandrea, a boat from Salerno is the shortest, calmest way in. Salerno airport now has flights too, which puts you close to the coast the moment you land.

Capri on the way. From Naples, the boat can stop at Capri for lunch or a swim and carry on to Positano in the afternoon. It turns a travel day into one of the best days of the trip.

Hotel to hotel. Splitting your stay between Positano and the Amalfi side? Check out of one hotel in the morning and spend the day on a crewed boat along the coast, ending at the next one. Your bags go ahead by car.

Coming from Rome. That is a long way by boat. For Rome I arrange a private car to the coast or a helicopter to the helipad at Scala, near Ravello, with a car waiting when you land.

Tell me the flight you are landing on and the hotel you are ending at, and I will pick the port, the boat and the timing.

Hotels on the water

The hotels where the boat makes the most sense

These four sit right on the sea, with their own beach clubs at the water’s edge. I have stayed at or toured every one of them.

Il San Pietro

Positano · from Naples

Built into the cliff just outside Positano, with a lift down to the sea. The hotel runs its own boats, including free shuttles into town, so you come home to quiet.

Villa Treville

Positano · from Naples

The most private rooms in Positano, a beach club on the rocks and three boats of its own. The free boat shuttles make Positano easy.

Hotel Santa Caterina

Amalfi · from Salerno

A five-minute walk from Amalfi, with an elevator down through the cliff to the beach club. Stairs one way, the elevator the other.

Borgo Santandrea

Conca dei Marini · from Salerno

A white modernist hotel stacked down the cliff to its own beach and dock. This is the view you get arriving by boat.

On the Amalfi side, Miramalfi has a beach club at the water too. Whether the boat pulls up at the hotel itself or at the nearest harbor depends on the sea that day; when it is the harbor, a car does the last few minutes. More on every hotel in my Amalfi Coast guide.

No. 03

Or fly in

The helicopter option

Best for · coming from Rome, short stays, any day the sea says no

If the boat is the scenic way in, the helicopter is the fast one. You lift off from Naples or Rome, fly down over the bay and land at the helipad in Scala, up in the hills near Ravello. A car is waiting on the pad and takes you down to your hotel.

Scala is the landing point. The closest helipad to these hotels is in Scala, up above Amalfi. From there it is about 20 to 25 minutes by car to most of these hotels, and much less to Ravello.

It solves Rome. Rome to the coast is a long drive. By helicopter it becomes a short flight, so you can leave Rome after breakfast and be at the pool for lunch.

A second way to skip the road. If the sea is too rough for the boat, the helicopter is the other way to avoid the drive. Weather decides both, so a car is always on standby.

Bags, sized up front. Helicopters take less luggage than a boat. Tell me what you are bringing, and the large cases go by road to meet you at the hotel.

No. 04

Good to know before you book

The honest fine print

Read this · before you commit to a date

None of this is a reason not to do it. It is what separates a smooth arrival from a stressful one.

The sea gets a vote. On a rough day the captain may change the route or call it off. I always have the car on standby, so a cancelled boat never costs you a hotel night.

Big luggage goes by road. A day boat handles a couple of cases comfortably. If you are travelling heavy, the large bags go ahead by car and are waiting in your room when you arrive.

Pricing is per boat, not per person. It depends on the distance, the size of the boat and any stops you add. Split between a family or two couples, it is a very different number than it sounds.

It is a season thing. Boat transfers run from spring through autumn. In the quiet months, and on any day the sea says no, I arrange a private car or the helicopter instead.

Book it early in summer. June through August, the good captains and the good boats are spoken for weeks out.

This is the part of an Amalfi trip I spend the most time on for clients, and the part you should never have to think about.

Going bigger

If you want the whole day on the water

A transfer is the practical end of this. The other end is a crewed boat for the day: Capri and the Faraglioni, a swim at the Li Galli islands off Positano, lunch at a beach restaurant you can only reach by sea, then back to your hotel at sunset.

If you are moving hotels, the two combine nicely. Check out in the morning, spend the day on the boat, and end it at your next hotel. If that is what you are picturing, say so in the note below and I will put boats in front of you.
