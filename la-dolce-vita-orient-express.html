I toured La Dolce Vita in Rome, from the private lounge to the dining car. Here’s what it’s really like on board, every journey and fare, and how I’d book it. If you’re thinking about it, the answer is do it.

Ask me about a journey See the journeys

Italy’s new Orient Express train, and one of the most fun things I’ve done in this industry.

Designed by Dimorestudio in the spirit of 1960s Italy, with a kitchen led by Heinz Beck, La Dolce Vita runs one- to four-night journeys out of Rome: to Venice, Tuscany’s wine country, Piedmont in truffle season and down to Sicily.

Below is my take from the tour, every current journey with fares, and the fine print I’d want to know before booking. To pair it with the Orient Express hotels in Rome and Venice, see my Orient Express in Italy trip.

Before you board

The La Dolce Vita lounge

La Dolce Vita has its own private terminal at Rome Ostiense, and it sets the tone for everything after.

You check in at the front desk, where your “boarding pass” and everything for the journey is waiting in a numbered pigeonhole behind the counter. Then it’s free-flowing Champagne and snacks in a lounge designed like the train itself: lacquered gold ceilings, travertine, velvet armchairs and a mosaic floor.

The lounge

The details

The buzz in the lounge before the train was so much fun. Everyone was excited, almost like Christmas morning, and I’ve never seen that in this industry. Arrive early and enjoy it. It’s part of the trip.

Which I’d pick

Rome to Venice, one night

For pairing La Minerva with Orient Express Venice, take one of the three journeys that end in Venice: Rome to Venice in December, the Carnival Party in February, or via Portofino from May. For a longer ride, the Wine or Truffle Route is two nights, and the Grand Tour does four.

Before you ask

La Dolce Vita questions

How much does La Dolce Vita Orient Express cost?

Fares are per person and vary by journey, cabin and date. Orient Express currently lists Rome to Venice and Rome, Portofino and Venice from €3,500 for one night, the Carnival Party from €4,440, the Italian Wine Route from €5,780, the Truffle Route from €6,560, Rome to Sicily and Venice & Tuscany in Winter from €9,440, and the five-day Grand Tour from €14,000. I quote exact fares for your dates, along with any current offers.

Can I take the train from Rome to Venice?

Yes. Three journeys run one way from Rome to Venice, each with one night on board: Rome to Venice via Bolgheri from December 2026, the Carnival Party in February 2027, and Rome, Portofino and Venice from May 2027. You step off in Venice, and Orient Express Venice is close to the station.

How many cabins are on the train?

Twelve Deluxe Cabins, eighteen Suites and one La Dolce Vita Suite, for up to 62 guests. It is small, so the best cabins on popular dates go first.

Is La Dolce Vita Orient Express worth it?

Yes. If you're thinking about doing it, the answer is do it. It is a unique experience, and the buzz in the lounge before boarding is unlike anything else I have seen in travel.

Are the cabins small?

Yes, and that is worth knowing going in. Every cabin has its own bathroom, but even the suites are compact and remind you that you are on a train. It shouldn't stop you from booking. It just sets your expectations correctly.

What should I pack?

A carry-on. For one to three nights on board you don't need much, and the cabins aren't built for big suitcases.

How many people are on the train?

Usually around 40 to 60 guests, with room for up to 62. Expect to be social and have fun.

What is the La Dolce Vita lounge like?

It is a private terminal for the train at Rome Ostiense. You check in at the front desk and collect your boarding pass, then enjoy free-flowing Champagne and snacks before boarding. Arrive early; the atmosphere is part of the experience.

What perks do I get booking through you?

At both Orient Express La Minerva and Orient Express Venice, my clients typically get daily breakfast for two, a $100 USD hotel credit, an upgrade on arrival and early check-in or late check-out when available, plus a VIP welcome. Signature Suite bookings at La Minerva can also include a 50-minute massage for two. Perks depend on the rate and are confirmed when we book.

Can we charter the whole train privately?

Yes, on request. The full train can be privatized for up to 62 guests, subject to availability and operational feasibility, with routes built around your group and bespoke menus. Smaller groups can privatize selected carriages.

Who does the food on the train?

The culinary program is led by Heinz Beck, the three-Michelin-star chef behind La Pergola in Rome.

Where does the train depart from?

Journeys start in Rome, from the La Dolce Vita lounge at Ostiense station. That is why I put Rome and La Minerva at the start of the trip.

Where should we stay before and after the train?

Journeys start in Rome, so I put clients at Orient Express La Minerva by the Pantheon first, then Orient Express Venice if your journey ends there. See how I put the three together on my Orient Express in Italy page.
