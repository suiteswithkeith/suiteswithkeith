Where You’ll Go

The Route

Flights

Getting to Athens

I don’t book commercial airfare, and I’ll tell you why: you can almost always do better yourself, and I’d rather put my time into the ground.

What I do instead is tell you when to buy, which routes actually work for a sixteen-night trip, and how to spend points well. Nina & Daniel flew into Athens on a single European connection and paid for business class with transferred points, at a redemption they’d have talked themselves out of on their own. I priced it, they booked it.

Arrival and departure transfers are mine. A driver is standing at the gate in Athens, and the return flight is timed against the last ferry off Santorini with real margin — not the two hours a booking site thinks is fine.

Getting Between the Islands

Ferries, private boats & helicopter transfers

Greek island transfers are their own kind of planning — ferry schedules shift with the wind, and the right connection turns a travel day into part of the trip. Here’s exactly how I moved Nina & Daniel between four islands in sixteen nights, from high-speed catamaran ferries to a private boat charter booked for comfort over convenience.

High-Speed Ferry

Athens → Sifnos

A Seajets high-speed catamaran from Piraeus Port, about 2 hours 30 minutes, with VIP seating booked for a smoother ride out of the capital.

Private Boat Transfer

Sifnos → Milos

Rather than work around a limited public ferry schedule, I arranged a private boat transfer directly between the two islands — a faster, more scenic crossing timed entirely around Nina & Daniel’s day.

High-Speed Ferry

Milos → Santorini

A second Seajets catamaran from Adamantas into Santorini’s old port, about 2 hours 30 minutes, with luggage and port transfers arranged on both ends.

Private Helicopter Charter

Any island, via Hoper

For clients who’d rather skip the sea altogether, I can book private helicopter transfers through Hoper — Athens to Santorini in under an hour, with the Cyclades laid out below.

Every transfer — ferry, private boat, or helicopter — is booked, confirmed, and timed around the rest of the day before a client ever leaves home.

Athens

Where You’ll Stay

Four Seasons Astir Palace, Athens

A private pine-covered peninsula on the Athens Riviera, with a beach club and villa pools, thirty minutes from the Acropolis.

The Room, and Why That One

Sea View Bungalow Suite

The Arion building is the grand one, but the bungalows sit down on the water with their own stretch of beach and a fraction of the foot traffic. On a honeymoon, the quiet is worth more than the lobby.

What came with it. Booked as a Four Seasons preferred partner: a space-available upgrade at check-in, breakfast for two daily, a property credit, and early check-in requested and noted on the reservation — along with a note on the file that this is a honeymoon, which is how you end up in the bungalow closer to the water. Same rate you’d pay booking direct.

Sifnos

Where You’ll Stay

Stamna

A small, design-forward perch above Apollonia, arranged with private port transfers, on one of Greece’s great food islands.

The Room, and Why That One

Pool Suite

Sifnos is a food island, and Stamna puts you within a short drive of every table worth sitting at. The suites with their own pool take the view out over the Cyclades; the entry rooms don’t, and the shared pool draws young families as readily as honeymooners.

What came with it. Booked through my Fora partnership: a space-available upgrade, daily breakfast, a hotel credit, and my direct line to the team on property. On a small hotel like this one that access matters more than the credit — it’s how the $25-per-bag laundry got queued up before arrival, halfway through a sixteen-night trip. Same rate you’d pay booking direct.

Milos

Where You’ll Stay

Erēma, a Member of Design Hotels

Quiet, design-forward, and close to the island’s volcanic beaches — more private estate than resort.

The Room, and Why That One

Horizon Suite with Private Pool

The room I’d book here myself: expansive indoor-outdoor living with uninterrupted Aegean views and real privacy. Erēma is the newest of the three hotels from the group behind Cosme and Parīlio, and the private-pool suites are what the architecture was actually designed around.

What came with it. A space-available upgrade, daily breakfast and a hotel credit through my Fora partnership — plus arrival timed with the property directly, so the room was ready off a morning boat from Sifnos rather than at three in the afternoon. Same rate you’d pay booking direct.

Stop 04 · Jun 17–21

Santorini

Where You’ll Stay

Andronis Boutique Hotel

Twenty-five cave-style suites and villas in the heart of Oia — the rooms first-time Santorini visitors are actually picturing.

The Room, and Why That One

Eternal Suite

Every room at Andronis Boutique has a plunge pool or jacuzzi, which is rare on the caldera and isn’t the case at the Canaves hotels. The Eternal Suite is the two-level one, and it’s the room I’d book for myself. Four nights in Oia at the end of the trip is enough to earn the splurge on the last leg.

What came with it. A space-available upgrade, daily breakfast and a hotel credit — and a table at Lycabettus, the hotel’s cliffside restaurant, held the same day the room was confirmed. It fills faster than the hotel does, and it is the single reservation on this trip most likely to be gone if you wait. Same rate you’d pay booking direct.

On the Ground

The part you’re actually paying for

Greek ferries cancel. The meltemi comes up and the afternoon boat doesn’t run. This happens on a real percentage of Cyclades trips, and it is the reason to have somebody.

When it happens, you don’t call the ferry company. You text me. I rebook the crossing or put you on a private boat, move the hotel night, push the dinner reservation and tell the next hotel you’re arriving late. You find out about it after it’s solved.

I’m reachable for the entire trip, not just business hours in Texas.

Plainly

What this one cost

Sixteen nights, two people, early June: roughly $30,000 to $38,000, covering four hotels, all ground and sea transfers, the private boat charter, guides and excursions, and the reservations. International flights are separate.

Most of the trips I plan land between $7,000 and $10,000 at the entry end. I work with a $500 per night hotel minimum. Hotel bookings are complimentary; full-trip planning starts at $500.

Not a Package

The same four islands, three ways

This itinerary isn’t a product. It’s one answer to one set of questions. Change the questions and it changes.

Ten Nights

Shorter

Drop Athens to two nights at the front, keep Sifnos and Santorini, cut Milos. Nothing feels rushed.

Twelve Nights

No ferries

Same islands, private boats and a Hoper helicopter between them. You buy back a full day of travel time, and the crossings become part of the trip.

Sixteen Nights

Quieter

Swap Santorini for Folegandros and finish at Gundari, on the cliffs, with almost nobody around. For couples who want the drama without the crowds.
