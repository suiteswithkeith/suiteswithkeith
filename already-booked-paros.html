No. 01

Dinner reservations

The handful of Naousa tables worth planning around

Booked · 3–6 weeks ahead in high season · Naousa fills even in October

Paros eats better than islands twice as famous, and the reason is Naousa: a little Venetian harbor ringed with tables where the fishing boats are almost at your elbow, and lanes behind it where the best cooking on the island hides without a view. The catch is that everyone who’s been knows it. Naousa books up even in October, so the tables that set the tone for a trip are a booking, not a walk-in. Tell me your dates and I’ll arrange the nights that matter, working with your hotel’s concierge where it helps.

Siparos, Santa Maria. On the water looking across at Naxos, and the seafood benchmark on the island. This is the long first-night dinner that sets the tone for the trip, and the first table I go after.

Yemeni, Naousa. The best cooking on Paros plate for plate — modern Greek in the lanes behind the harbor, without a view and better for it.

Sigi Ikthios, Naousa. Tucked into the old harbor with the fishing boats almost at your table. The evening there is the point as much as the fish.

Santa Pacou, Lefkes. Dinner up in the marble village, a world away from the harbor scene. Timed after the sunset walk through Lefkes it’s the most memorable evening on the island — and it belongs on your village day, below.

Parostia at Cosme. One of my favorite restaurants on Paros and the right last night. If you’re staying at Cosme, it’s an evening with nowhere to drive afterward.

Mimi Kakushi at Luura. New in August 2026: the Dubai contemporary-Japanese room’s first Greek outpost, on a cliff facing the Antiparos sunset. Nothing else on Paros is attempting anything like it, and I expect it to be the hardest table on the island this season.

One good rule: two big dinners across a four-night stay, and let the rest be long lunches — To Balconi tou Aki in Aliki is the one I’d build a day around. Send me your dates and I’ll tell you which nights are already tight and what I can still get.

No. 02

Your day on the water

The Antiparos sail, booked before it goes

Best for · couples, anyone who came for the water, groups of up to ten

The water on the Antiparos side of Paros is the clearest I know in the Cyclades, and the only way to reach the best of it is by boat. The sails run out of Piso Livadi on the east coast — a working fishing harbor twenty minutes from Naousa, with none of the marina scrum you get on the bigger islands. This is the one booking on Paros I’d protect, because the good boats and the good weather windows both go first.

Sail to Antiparos & Despotiko. The full day. Out of Piso Livadi under sail at 9:30, down into the Antiparos Strait, anchored in the Blue Lagoon, then right around South Antiparos with a full lunch cooked and served on deck, and a last swim off South Paros on the way home. Fully private for the two of you, or shared with up to ten — your call.

The evening sail. The shorter version, and lovely on its own night: sails up at 6:00pm, a drink in hand, a swim in a sheltered bay, then dinner served on deck as the sun drops. Back at the dock by dusk, so it never collides with a table in Naousa.

A private boat, priced for the boat. Three boats run the private days, and the rate is for the whole boat rather than per person — the same whether it’s the two of you or a full group, with skipper, fuel and VAT included. I’ll match the boat to your budget rather than the other way around.

Antiparos, the easy way. If the sail isn’t your day, the little car ferry shuttles from Pounda every half hour and takes ten minutes. No booking, no drama — one long main street of galleries and good tavernas, the cave in the hills above, and Soros beach on the way back.

The Meltemi is real. Summer wind can move a sail with little notice, so I place the boat early in your stay with room to shift it — which you can’t do once you’ve prepaid a fixed date online.

Tell me your dates and who’s coming and I’ll hold the right boat for the right morning. The full menu, with what each one costs, is in my Paros excursions guide.

No. 03

Beaches & down days

Choose the beach by mood, not by whichever one your hotel sits on

Best for · slow days, long lunches, a break from the car

This is why Paros over the bigger names: beaches you choose by mood rather than by geography, and none of them more than forty minutes from anywhere else. Nothing here needs more than the car and a loose sense of when lunch should be — but the ones with beds are still worth reserving in July and August, and I’d rather you didn’t find that out at eleven in the morning.

Kolymbithres. Wind-carved granite coves and water you can see straight through — unlike any other beach in the Cyclades, and a short hop across the bay from Naousa. Go early, before the boats arrive.

Crios Beach Club. The island’s most relaxed beachfront setting for swimming and a slow lunch. Nothing to do but stay put until the light changes — the day that asks nothing of you at all, and the beds I reserve first.

Lageri and Santa Maria. Lageri for calm, shallow water and almost nobody on it; Santa Maria when you want loungers and a bar. Both face Naxos across the channel, and both are ten minutes from Naousa.

If you’re at Cosme or Parīlio, you already have one. Cosme’s private beach club sits directly in front of the hotel, and Parīlio guests use it for free — some of the best days on Paros are the ones where you never leave.

One rule for Paros: rent the car for every day you’re there. Roads are good, parking is easy outside the two towns, and the best beaches sit at the end of short dirt tracks. I’ll have it waiting at the airport or the port.

The village day

The day Paros does better than any island I know

Real villages with real life in them, a mountain in the middle, and a seafood lunch on the south coast between them. This is the day I’d build first — in the right order, with the two tables held before you land.

01Lefkes, in the morning. Start in the mountain village — marble lanes, no sea, no crowds. Wander before the day heats up, and save the sunset for the drive back. Marble lanes · around 10:00am
02Lunch at To Balconi tou Aki, Aliki. South to the fishing village of Aliki for a seafood lunch at one of my favorite tavernas on the island — the lunch I’d build the whole day around. Seafood on the water · around 1:30pm
03A swim at Lageri. Calm, shallow, clear water and almost nobody on it. The afternoon that asks nothing of you. Calm water · the afternoon

This is the day the rental car earns its keep — you drive it, at your own pace. I hold the two tables, put it on the day the wind is least likely to cooperate with a boat, and give you the route in the order that works.

No. 04

The Athens add-on

Two or three nights, not a layover

Best for · bookending the trip, food lovers, first-time Greece

Most people treat Athens as a gateway to the islands. I think that’s a mistake. Give yourself two or three nights and you’ll discover one of Europe’s most exciting food cities, incredible luxury hotels, and neighborhoods that feel completely different from one another. It’s the perfect way to begin or end a trip through Greece.

Put it at the end, not the beginning. Flying home out of Athens gives you a buffer against a cancelled ferry or a delayed island flight — and you arrive in the city already unwound.

Two nights is the minimum that works. One night is an airport hotel with extra steps. Three is genuinely great.

Book the Acropolis early. Timed entry, and the first slot of the morning is the only one worth having in summer.

Rules I’d give any client

Book the flight before the ferry. Paros’ airport is small, and the Athens flights sell out well before the boats do in season.
Never book the last ferry of the day. If it cancels, there’s nothing behind it.
Don’t fly home the same day you sail. Build in a night. The Aegean does not care about your itinerary.
Two islands per week. That’s the limit. Three islands in seven nights is three travel days.
