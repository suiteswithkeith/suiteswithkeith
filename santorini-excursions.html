The Shape Of It

The island, and where everything sits

Santorini is a crescent around a flooded volcano. Everything with a view sits along the west rim — Fira, Imerovigli, Oia — and everything else, the vineyards, the ruins and the black-sand beaches, is on the gentle slope behind. Tap any pin for what it is.

Private transfers

What I arrange by default for the airport and the ferry port. Athinios is a steep, chaotic switchback and the taxi queue there is genuinely unpleasant.

A car for one day

Worth it for the inland day — wineries, Pyrgos, the south coast. Not worth it for the rest: parking in Oia and Fira is close to impossible in season.

Taxis are scarce

There are only a few dozen on the whole island and none you can hail. Have your hotel call ahead, or pre-book the driver for a dinner at the other end of the island.

Oia to Ammoudi

Three hundred steps down, shared with donkeys. Walk down if you like, but book a car for the way back up — especially after lunch and wine.

On the water

You cannot understand Santorini from the rim — you have to be under it. Every boat below runs roughly the same circuit out of Ammoudi or the Old Port: along the caldera wall, out to the volcano and the hot springs, past the lighthouse to the red and white beaches, and back at sunset. What changes is how many people are on board with you.

Shared · 5 hours · Book ahead

The classic caldera cruise

A catamaran with a group, the full circuit, meal and drinks on board. Morning departures are calmer and cheaper; the sunset run is the one people remember. Boat size is what you’re really choosing — from fifty-five people down to fourteen.

If it’s your first time and you’re happy in company, this is the sensible booking.

From $87 per person

A smaller catamaran

Same route, fourteen or twenty guests instead of fifty-five, more space on deck and a proper cooked meal rather than a buffet. The difference in the day is bigger than the difference in the price.

The volcano and the hot springs

Nea Kameni is still live — a short, hot climb over black rock to the crater rim, then a swim in the rust-colored sulphur water beside it. Wear something you don’t mind staining.

Lunch · Book ahead

Armeni, by taxi boat

Down the steps to Ammoudi, a small boat around the headland, and lunch on the rocks at a table almost in the water. Barely an excursion, and still one of my favorite meals on the island.

Bookable · Two

Wine, and the island behind the view

Assyrtiko is grown here in a way it isn’t anywhere else — vines coiled into low baskets on the ground, no trellis, no irrigation, roots deep in volcanic ash and pumice. The wine that comes out is bone-dry, saline and unmistakable. Vassaltis is the one I send people to: modern, serious, and small enough that you’re looked after properly.

1 hour · Book ahead

Vassaltis tasting

Four wines and four bites, each pairing made for the glass it comes with — current vintages plus a couple of rarer vinifications. A crash course in Assyrtiko and the easiest thing here to slot into an afternoon.

From $44 per person

Lunch to share, at the vineyard

Three courses meant to be shared, paired with tastings of three wines, at a table looking down the vine rows to the sea. Offered daily, and my pick if you want the wine to be lunch rather than an errand before it.

Half day

Pyrgos, on foot

The old capital, stacked up the highest hill on the island inside its castle walls. Ten minutes of climbing lanes gets you a view over both coasts at once, and almost nobody is up there.

Half day

Akrotiri

A Bronze Age town buried by the eruption and dug back out — three-story houses, paved streets, drainage, frescoes. It’s under a roof, which makes it the right call on a hot day or a rare wet one.

Afternoon

The black-sand south

Vlychada for the wind-carved cliffs and near-empty sand, Perissa if you’d rather have a lounger and a taverna. Both are a different island from the rim — flat, hot, and gloriously ordinary.

Santorini · On Foot

Three walks, and one of them is essential

There is almost no shade anywhere on this island. All three of these are morning walks — start by eight, carry water, and have a car meet you at the far end rather than walking back.

3–4 hours · 10 km · Moderate

Fira to Oia, along the rim

The signature walk, and the only way to understand the shape of the island: the whole crescent of the caldera on your left, Firostefani and Imerovigli, then a long empty stretch over the headland before Oia appears. Undulating and exposed — the hard part is the sun, not the climb.

2 hours return · 4 km · Moderate–steep

Ancient Thera, above Kamari

Switchbacks up the Mesa Vouno headland to a Greek and Roman city strung along a ridge 350 metres above the sea, with Kamari on one side and Perissa on the other. Almost nobody walks it — and you can drive most of the way if you’d rather.

20 minutes down · 300 steps · Easy down, hard up

Oia down to Ammoudi

Not really a hike, but the best twenty minutes of walking on the island: the steps zigzag down the red cliff to a tiny fishing port with tavernas built onto the rock. Go down for a late lunch, and take a car back up.

Santorini

Where to watch the sunset

The castle at Oia is famous, and by seven it is shoulder to shoulder with several thousand people. Every one of these is better.

Your own terrace

Genuinely the best answer on the caldera side. A bottle, no crowd, and the light doing the work. Any hotel I book you into on the rim faces west for exactly this reason.

Imerovigli, from Skaros

The short walk out to the old rock fortress puts you on the highest point of the rim with Oia in profile to the north. Twenty minutes from the village and a fraction of the people.

From a boat

Looking back at the cliffs with the villages lighting up along the top of them. Every caldera cruise is timed for this, and it is a completely different picture from the land version.

Pyrgos, inland

The contrarian pick and my own favorite — from the castle you see the sun go down over the whole island rather than just the water, with the caldera villages glowing on the far ridge.

The Tables

Where I’d eat

At Canaves Epitome

Omnia

One of my favorite meals in all of Greece, and not on the caldera — which tells you how good the cooking is.

At Andronis Luxury Suites, Oia

Lycabettus

The big-occasion table, carved into the cliff face. Usually booked out — if you’re staying at Andronis I’ll have the concierge team try, and it’s worth asking early.

At The Grace, Imerovigli

Varoulko

Beautiful fine dining, seafood-led, on the quietest stretch of the rim. The most elegant dinner on the island without being stiff about it.

Exo Gonia

Metaxi Mas

Very authentic Greek dining, inland and unfussy, and where the island itself eats. No caldera view, which is exactly why it’s good. Tables go weeks ahead.

Ammoudi Bay, below Oia

Ammoudi Fish Tavern

An authentic Greek fish tavern on the water at the bottom of the steps — a super cute spot, and the easy lunch on a day you’re not doing anything else.

How I’d spend them

Three nights is the right length for Santorini. One day on the water, one day inland, and one day that asks nothing of you at all.

Day One · Settle in

Arrive, do nothing, and let the island introduce itself. Walk Oia in the late afternoon before the sunset crowd arrives, then down the steps to Ammoudi for fish on the water and a car back up.

Dinner: Ammoudi Fish Tavern

Day Two · On the water

The caldera cruise, all day — volcano, hot springs, the red and white beaches, and the cliffs from below. Back late in the afternoon, and keep the evening close to home.

Don’t miss: the swim at the volcano, even if the water is cold.

Day Three · Inland

A car for the day. Akrotiri in the morning while it’s cool, lunch and wine at Vassaltis, then Pyrgos for the late light. Dinner inland at Metaxi Mas, which is where the island actually eats.

Dinner: Metaxi Mas, booked well ahead
