The best luxury hotels in Italy for families with teenagers

Teenagers don’t need a kids club. They need room of their own, something to do and a hotel that isn’t built for honeymooners. These are the ones I’d book.

Updated November 2026

A lot of Italy’s most beautiful hotels are made for two. That’s exactly the problem with teenagers.

Lake Como’s intimate villas, the Amalfi Coast’s cliff hotels and Rome’s palazzi are mostly built for couples. Book one of those with two sixteen-year-olds and everyone is unhappy by day three.

So for this list I looked for three things: space (two- and three-bedroom suites, villas, or suites that connect), things to do (real pools, tennis, a proper gym, boats, mountains) and a hotel that welcomes families rather than tolerates them. Each pick below is a hotel I’ve reviewed on this site, one per region, with the alternative I’d consider.

The list

Where I’d send a family with teens

01 · Venice · Giudecca

Airelles Palladio

The best family hotel in Venice, and my #1 in Italy

Most of Venice’s great hotels are palazzi without a pool. Airelles is a resort. It has a 25-meter pool in nearly a hectare of walled gardens, an indoor pool in a three-level spa with a fitness area, four restaurants and the hotel boat to San Marco, included in the rate, about five minutes each way. You can do the city in the morning and the pool in the afternoon.

Right for
Families who want Venice without giving up a resort
Room I’d book
Villa Mason.Three bedrooms, a private garden and its own pool, about $15,000 to $25,000 a night. For a multigenerational trip, the four-bedroom Suite del Doge.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Everyone gets a bedroom. Villa Mason is entirely private and connects to one of the historic buildings.

Lots included. Breakfast, the minibar, the boat, the spa and the pools are all in the rate.

Alternative: Hotel Cipriani. The biggest pool in Venice and a clay court. Its mood leans adult, so it suits older children best.

The verdict

The best family hotel in Venice, without question. Nothing else in the city gives teenagers this much to do.

02 · Lake Como · Tremezzo

Grand Hotel Tremezzo

Lively, with lots to do

Lake Como is at its best as a romantic stop, and most of its great hotels are intimate villas built for couples. Tremezzo is the exception. It’s lively, with three pools (the garden pool is my favorite), a beautiful clay tennis court, five restaurants and the hotel’s own boats. One warning: it’s built into the hillside, so there are a lot of stairs.

Right for
Active families who want a big, lively hotel
Room I’d book
A junior suite or above.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Value alternative: the Lake Como EDITION. Its two-bedroom suite is around $5,500 a night, good value for a family sharing one suite. It also has one of the best hotel gyms I’ve seen, with free weights and a squat rack.

Or the Mandarin Oriental. Its scale works for families, and its floating infinity pool is on my list of the world’s best pools.

Tennis players: Villa d’Este. Eight clay courts, and it works well for multigenerational groups.

The verdict

My pick for families on Lake Como, with three pools and plenty of space.

03 · Florence

Four Seasons Hotel Firenze

A garden and a pool in the middle of the city

Florence is a city of museums, and teenagers have a limit. The Giardino della Gherardesca gives you acres of green, a real pool and room to spread out, all a ten-minute walk from the Duomo. The gym is spread over two full floors. After the Uffizi, everyone can go back to the pool.

Right for
Families who want the city and a pool

Or Collegio alla Querce. Still very family friendly, with room to breathe.

04 · Tuscany · Volterra

Borgo Pignano

Your own villa, with a resort around it

Around nine private villas are spread across the estate, from two to five bedrooms. You get the privacy of a villa, plus two pools, a separate kids’ pool, tennis, a fantastic gym and a beautiful spa. It’s a quiet country estate and a bit remote, so it suits families who want space and a slower pace.

Families who want a villa without giving up hotel service

Alternative: Castelfalfi. A huge main pool, a spa with an infinity pool, four restaurants, a kids club and plenty of family room layouts. A great option if you don’t want to pay Rosewood, COMO or Belmond prices.

Or Il Borro. Its newer rooms, about three minutes away by car, have their own restaurant and pool and suit families better than the original borgo.

05 · Amalfi Coast · Amalfi

Hotel Santa Caterina

The family hotel on a coast built for couples

Most Amalfi Coast hotels are made for two. Santa Caterina is where families come back for generations. Casa Marchesa, a 12-room annex about 300 meters away, has a three-bedroom villa with its own pool. Down through the gardens, or by elevator, there’s a beach club right on the sea, and there’s a sea-view gym.

Right for
Slow stays, families, multigenerational trips
Room I’d book
The Casa Marchesa three-bedroom villa.A natural fit for families and friends traveling together.

Keith’s notes

The sea, not just the view. The lido puts the swimming at the bottom of the cliff, not a drive away.

Skip the couples’ hotels. Caruso, Villa Treville and Miramalfi are built for couples.

The verdict

My #2 hotel in Italy, and the one I’d book on the Amalfi Coast for a family.

06 · Dolomites · San Cassiano

Aman Rosa Alpina

Ski, hike, and an Aman that welcomes children

The Dolomites are the active choice: skiing in winter and hiking in summer. Aman Rosa Alpina is equally good for families and couples, which isn’t always true of an Aman. It isn’t ski-in, ski-out, but there’s a ski room and the lifts are about two minutes away by shuttle.

Right for
Skiers, hikers, families who want Aman calm

Keith’s notes

Come in winter or summer. In October it was a ghost town.

True ski-in, ski-out: COMO Alpina. One of my picks for families, and in summer the trails start at your door.

Best town: Gardena Grödnerhof. In Ortisei, for hiking, eating out in town and bringing children.

The verdict

If skiing and hiking are the point, or you’re bringing children, pick Aman.

07 · Italian Riviera · Santa Margherita

Grand Hotel Miramare

Beach club, Portofino by boat, and suites that combine

It works very well for families. Certain suites can be combined behind one closed door, so you get two proper suites as one private unit. There’s a pool, one of the largest beach clubs in Liguria and Portofino by boat for lunch. Sea-view rooms run around $1,000 to $1,500 a night.

The verdict

The honest part

Beautiful hotels I wouldn’t book with teens

These are some of my favorite hotels in Italy. They’re just not built for this trip.

Il Sereno, Lake Como. It’s not a family hotel. I’d only send families with older teenagers who are happy by the pool.

Passalacqua, Lake Como. It says it’s family friendly, but to me it’s a couple’s retreat. For families: Tremezzo or the Mandarin Oriental.

Forestis, Dolomites. The hotel is for guests aged 14 and over, and the tower suites on the same floor don’t connect. Families can book the five-bedroom Forestis Villa instead, with its own team, pool and spa, from about €25,000 a night.

Aman Venice. Two three-bedroom apartments can house a family, but the hotel is built for calm, and there’s no pool.

Rome. None of my Rome picks is a family hotel. The closest is Hotel Eden, whose top suites can be combined for families.

Before you ask

The questions I get

What is the best luxury hotel in Italy for families with teenagers?

Airelles Palladio in Venice. It has three pools, a three-level spa with a fitness area and the three-bedroom Villa Mason with its own pool, and it’s my number one hotel in Italy.

Is Lake Como good for teenagers?

It can be, at the right hotel. Most of the lake’s great hotels are intimate villas built for couples. Book Grand Hotel Tremezzo, with three pools, a clay court and boats, or the two-bedroom suite at the Lake Como EDITION.

Which Italian hotels are adults only or not good for families?

Forestis is for guests 14 and over. Il Sereno and Passalacqua are couples’ hotels, and Aman Venice has no pool.

What is the best family villa in Italy?

Borgo Pignano in Tuscany, with private villas of two to five bedrooms and the estate’s pools, tennis, gym and spa.

Which Amalfi Coast hotel is best for families?

Hotel Santa Caterina. Its Casa Marchesa annex has a three-bedroom villa with its own pool, and there’s a beach club on the sea.

Where should we go in Italy with teenagers who ski or hike?

The Dolomites. Aman Rosa Alpina for suites and calm, COMO Alpina for ski-in, ski-out, and Gardena Grödnerhof in Ortisei for the best town.
