Orient Express or the Danieli?

Two of Venice’s big hotel stories this year. Orient Express is a 15th-century palazzo after an eight-year restoration; the Danieli has reopened as a Four Seasons next to the Doge’s Palace. Here is how I would choose.

Toured both · October 2026

Choose Orient Express

My pick of the two

An eight-year restoration, some seventy kinds of marble, restored ceilings and Murano chandeliers, on a quiet canal corner in Cannaregio. It is almost overwhelming how beautiful it is.

The question behind the question

Why I would book Orient Express

I toured both on the same day in October 2026, along with Airelles Palladio and Aman Venice. Orient Express wowed me. “It is almost overwhelming how beautiful this property is” is what I said out loud on the tour, and I meant it.

I liked the Danieli and think it is a great option, but it did not quite wow me the same way. The lobby and the rooftop are the stars; the rooms are classic and understated.

Book the Danieli if being at San Marco is the point, or if you collect Four Seasons stays. A bigger hotel also means better odds of an upgrade.

The detail

What to know before you choose

Location: quiet corner or center stage

Orient Express sits on a calm canal corner in Cannaregio, away from the San Marco crowds and an easy walk into the city. It is close to Santa Lucia, so it works well if you arrive by train from Florence, Milan or the Dolomites.

The Danieli is right on the Riva degli Schiavoni, steps from St. Mark’s. That can be a plus or a minus: you are in the middle of everything, and it is busy right in front of your door.

Design versus the scene

At Orient Express, it is the mix: dark wood with some seventy kinds of marble, book-matched in the bathrooms, restored painted and coffered ceilings, and Murano chandeliers. Everywhere I looked, something new caught my eye.

At the Danieli, the Gothic atrium is the great see-and-be-seen room in Venice, and Terrazza Danieli on the roof looks over the Salute, the Doge’s Palace and the lagoon. Even if you stay elsewhere, go for a drink or dinner.

Rooms and service

At Orient Express, ask for the piano nobile, the noble floor one level above the ground floor, where the ceilings are highest. At about 47 rooms, service feels personal.

The Danieli’s rooms are classic; book a lagoon view, which is what makes them. At about 120 rooms, service is less personal, which is where my relationships with the team help.

Neither has a pool

Both are palazzo hotels without a pool, and the Danieli’s spa is still coming, due in late 2026. If a pool or a real spa matters, look at Airelles Palladio on Giudecca, my number one hotel in Italy.

Before you ask

The questions I get

Which is better, Orient Express or the Danieli?

For most travelers, Orient Express. The eight-year restoration is jaw-dropping, and its corner of Cannaregio is quiet but convenient. Choose the Danieli if you want to be at San Marco, with its famous Gothic lobby and the rooftop restaurant.

Do either of them have a pool?

No. Neither has a pool. For a pool in Venice, look at Airelles Palladio on Giudecca, with an outdoor pool in the gardens and an indoor pool in its spa.

Which is closer to the train station?

Orient Express, in Cannaregio, close to Santa Lucia station. The Danieli is on the Riva degli Schiavoni next to the Doge’s Palace, at the other end of the city.

Is the Danieli a Four Seasons now?

Yes. It reopened as the Danieli, A Four Seasons Hotel, in July 2026. Its spa is due to open in late 2026.

Which room should I book at each?

At Orient Express, a room or suite on the piano nobile, where the ceilings are highest. At the Danieli, a lagoon-view room or suite looking across to San Giorgio Maggiore.
