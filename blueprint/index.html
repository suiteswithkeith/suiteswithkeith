The brand, as it actually is

You are not running an advisor site.
You are running a publishing house.

I read the whole repository before writing a word of this. The thing that jumped out is not in your copy — it is in your file tree. You have 123 individual hotel pages, a Python content generator, ten private client proposals, and a printed magazine with a second issue in the pipeline. The median luxury travel advisor has six pages and an Instagram grid.

The moat

Amex Fine Hotels + Resorts can match your upgrade. Chase can match your resort credit. Any of the thousands of Fora and Virtuoso advisors can match your commission split. None of them can tell a couple which of Cosme and Parilio to book, because none of them slept in both.

Perks are a commodity with a public price list. Firsthand, dated, comparative judgment is not. The site should be organised so that a stranger understands this within one screen — today it takes about four.

What is diluting it

Four typefaces. Playfair, Cinzel, Cormorant and Jost, split across two unrelated themes — a cream site and a navy magazine that look like two companies.

The flagship is in a rented frame. Issue No. 01 renders inside an Issuu iframe: someone else’s chrome, invisible to Google, slow to load, impossible to link into.

The evidence is invisible. Nothing on a hotel page states whether you have personally stayed there. That single fact is the whole reason to choose you.

The structural consequence

Your only conversion path today is a form at the bottom of a long page, and your stated response time is “within a couple of days.” That works for the Honeymooners. It loses the Rescue outright and irritates the Habitual — the two segments with the highest margin.

Fix: a persistent inquiry affordance that is always one tap away, and a triage question as the first field — when do you travel? Anything inside 30 days routes to a different, faster promise than anything beyond it. One extra select element; a materially different business.

The first three seconds

The feeling is relief, not wanderlust

Every competitor sells wanderlust, and they outspend you on it. Wanderlust is also the wrong emotion: a visitor who wants to feel wonder goes to Instagram. A visitor on an advisor’s site is already sold on the trip and is now anxious about getting it wrong. The feeling to engineer is someone has already done this work, and better than I could. Why the number, and not a view

“Luxury travel, tailored to you” is what all three competitor tiers say. It is unfalsifiable, so it is unbelievable. 123 is checkable, and a visitor knows instinctively that nobody fabricates a number that specific. It also does something no adjective can: it makes the visitor feel behind. That is the deference.

Generate it from the file count at build time so it is never stale, and never wrong.

Hard rules for the hero

No carousel. A rotating hero says you could not decide, and it destroys the largest contentful paint.

No drone shot of the caldera. It is the single most reproduced image in travel and it signals stock, not presence.

No “Start Planning.” It is a system verb. “Tell me where you’re going” is a person asking a question, which is what the visitor actually wants.

The test: a stranger, at 3.0 seconds, can say out loud what you do and why you are different. If they cannot, the hero has failed regardless of how it looks.

The section that decides everything

Nobody converts on a hero. They only fail to.

The hero cannot close anyone — a honeymoon is a five-figure decision made over weeks. What it can do is lose them, and it does that in about four seconds. So the job is not persuasion. It is removing every reason to leave, then asking for something so small that saying yes costs nothing.

What they were doing sixty seconds ago

Four traffic sources, four different mental states. The mistake is writing one hero for the average of them — the average visitor does not exist. Write for the second column, because it is the largest and the coldest.

Instagram · warm Scrolling, half-bored “Is this a real business or a content account?” Low intent, high trust. Already likes you. Needs to learn you actually book trips — many followers do not know that.
Google · cold Comparing hotels in eleven tabs “Is this another affiliate blog?” High intent, zero trust. Arrives mid-research and deeply pattern-matched against SEO spam. The hardest and most valuable arrival.
Referral · hot Told a name by a friend “Am I in the right place?” Highest intent on the site. Needs confirmation and a contact route, nothing else. Do not sell to this person — they are already sold.
Direct · returning Thinking about it since last week “What was the next step again?” Came back deliberately. The hero should not re-pitch them; it should present the action immediately.

Consequence: the referral and the returning visitor need the action visible, not argued for. The Google arrival needs proof before anything else. These are compatible only if the proof is the headline — which is what all three test versions below do.

The one promise that matters

It is not “I will plan your trip.” It is not “exclusive perks” — Amex sells those too. The promise underneath every testimonial on your site is this:

You will not get this wrong, and you will not have to become an expert to avoid it.

Every headline candidate is measured against that sentence. If it does not deliver it, it is decoration.

The promise you are currently making

“Get In Touch” appears 182 times across the site. It is the dominant call to action by a wide margin, and it is a verb with no outcome attached — it asks the visitor to imagine what happens next and to want it enough to act. Nobody does that work for you.

The inquiry form sits near 500vh with five visible fields, and the line directly above the submit button promises a reply “within a couple of days.” That is the last thing a visitor reads before deciding. For the Rescue segment it is disqualifying.

The action, within ten seconds

Be honest about what ten seconds can buy. It cannot buy a five-field form with name, email, dates, occasion and a paragraph about the trip. It can buy one keystroke of commitment.

The primary button opens a single field, not the form:

Where and when?

One input. Free text. “Santorini in June” is a complete answer. Name and email are asked after that field is filled, when the visitor has already invested and abandoning feels like a loss. The remaining fields never appear at all — you ask them in the reply, which is a conversation rather than a form.

This is the highest-confidence recommendation in the document. Progressive disclosure on a first-touch inquiry reliably beats a full form, and your current form is long, late, and preceded by a two-day promise.

The supporting image

Subject: an interior at dusk — a lit lamp, an open door to the sea, a room that reads as occupied. A book face-down, a towel, an unmade corner. Human evidence.

Composition: weight the subject to one third and leave a naturally dark, quiet zone for the type. Choose a photograph that already has that zone rather than manufacturing one with a heavy scrim — an overlay past about 35% is how a good photograph gets ruined in service of legibility.

Spec: AVIF under 180KB, 1600px max, srcset at 640 / 1024 / 1600, explicit width and height, fetchpriority="high", preloaded, and a real <img> — never a CSS background.

Never: a drone shot of the caldera, an infinity pool from behind, or a couple in matching linen walking away from camera. Each one is a signal that the image was bought rather than taken.

The two buttons

Hover and press these — they carry the exact specification from the interaction section below.

Primary — “Tell me where you’re going.” A person asking a question, not a system verb. It states exactly what happens on click, and it is answerable in four words, which is what makes the one-field prompt behind it feel inevitable rather than abrupt.

Secondary — “See the 123 hotels.” Not “Learn more.” The secondary button exists for the majority who are not ready to speak to anyone, and it should send them into the proof, not into an About page. It also quietly restates the number for anyone who skipped the headline.

How to test this honestly

A solo advisor site almost certainly does not have the traffic for a clean simultaneous split — at typical volumes a true A/B on inquiry rate would need months to reach significance, and you would ship nothing while waiting.

Run them sequentially instead. Three to four weeks each, same season, same ad spend. It is a weaker method and you should treat the result as a strong hint rather than a proof, but it is the honest option at this scale.

Better: test on the funnel page. greece-last-minute.html already concentrates Instagram traffic onto one page with one intent. Variants land there faster and cleaner than on the homepage.

What to measure

Primary: inquiries per thousand sessions. The only number that pays.

Secondary: share of sessions scrolling past 100vh — this isolates whether the hero earned the next screen, and it moves long before the primary metric does, which makes it the one you can actually read in four weeks.

Qualitative, and most valuable: the share of inquiries that name a specific hotel. It tells you whether the judgment is landing, and it is the leading indicator that version C is working.

Do not measure: time on page or bounce. On a hero, a fast exit and a fast conversion look identical to both.

Change three — the photography rule that saves money

Stop showing the hotel’s photographs. They are shot by a crew with a budget you cannot match, they appear identically on Booking, Amex and the property’s own site, and they prove nothing about you.

Show your frames instead. The unmade bed at 7am. The breakfast tray. The key on the console. A corridor at 6pm. Interiors, not vistas. Evidence photography, not brochure photography.

This is strategically stronger and cheaper. An imperfect photograph that could only have been taken by someone standing in the room outperforms a flawless one that could have come from anywhere — because the imperfection is the proof.

Grid: 1180px container, 12 columns, 40px gutters. Sharp corners throughout — radius 2px on inputs only. Section rhythm 96px desktop, 56px mobile. All retained from your current system.

Interaction design

How the site moves

Across all 193 pages you currently have three keyframe animations and three intersection observers — effectively no motion at all. That is a far better place to start from than the opposite problem, because restraint is the hard part and you have not spent anything yet. You also already honour prefers-reduced-motion in 32 places, which is real discipline. The task is to add four behaviours and refuse the rest.

Personality, translated into physics

Brand adjectives are useless to an engineer until they become numbers. These five are the ones your own writing and client feedback keep landing on — here is what each one costs in milliseconds.

AssuredNothing hesitates, nothing bounces back. A confident thing arrives once and stays.No spring, no overshoot, no elastic easing. Ever.
UnhurriedThe brand does not rush the visitor, so the interface does not either — but slow is not the same as laggy.Entrances at the long end (520ms). Responses to input always under 180ms.
WeightedThings settle rather than snap. Weight is what reads as expensive; nothing cheap feels heavy.One decelerating curve everywhere: cubic-bezier(.2,.6,.2,1)
QuietNo element announces itself. If a visitor notices the animation, the animation failed.Travel distances of 8px, never 40px. Opacity does most of the work.
PreciseA hotel inspector’s brand. No organic wobble, no randomised delays, no drift.Fixed 60ms stagger, capped at five siblings. No randomness anywhere.

Where motion carries meaning

The stamp drawing itself. The one signature moment. Verification is the brand thesis, so the mark that asserts it earns an animation nothing else gets.

The hero sequence. Only on first visit in a session. It paces the argument so the number lands before the action appears.

Filter results reordering. On the Index, motion is genuinely informational — it shows that the set changed rather than that the page reloaded.

Press feedback. The only motion that is strictly functional. It confirms a tap was received, which on mobile is the difference between one tap and three.

Where motion only costs

Reveal on every element. When everything animates in, the page feels like it is still loading. Reveal containers, not their contents.

Anything scroll-linked. Parallax and scrub effects tie frame rate to scroll and are the single most common cause of jank on mid-range Android.

Counters that count up. “123” animating from zero delays the most important fact on the site for 800ms to perform having a fact.

Page transitions longer than 200ms. Any navigation delay is a tax paid by the visitors who already decided to go — your most engaged ones.

The baseline: things that never move

Navigation and logo. Prices and fees. The verification stamp once drawn. Form labels, validation messages and error text. Table data. Any block of running prose. The hero on a repeat visit.

The governing rule: if a visitor could plausibly be reading it, it does not move. Motion on text the eye is already tracking is not styling — it is an interruption, and it is the fastest way to make an expensive site feel cheap.

The corollary is a layout rule, not an animation one: nothing may shift because an asset arrived late. That is what the intrinsic dimensions on all 768 images buy you, and it is why Phase 0 comes before any of this.

Technology

Your architecture is right. Your build step is missing.

The temptation with a redesign is a rewrite into React. Do not. This is a content site with almost no application logic, it is free to host, and 193 pages of hand-tuned HTML is a genuine asset. The problem is not that the site is static — it is that nothing stands between you and the HTML, so every image ships unoptimised and every page repeats itself.

Performance budget

LCP under 1.5s on 4G · CLS under 0.05 · JS under 40KB compressed · hero image under 180KB AVIF · total page weight under 900KB · Lighthouse 95+ on all four.

Enforce it in CI. A budget nobody checks is a wish.

The migration is incremental

Astro can emit into the existing directory structure, so migrated pages and untouched legacy HTML coexist on the same domain throughout. There is never a cutover weekend, never a moment where the site is half-broken, and any phase can ship on its own.

Build order

Triage first. Design second.

This is the one genuinely sequential part of the blueprint, so it is the only part numbered. Phase 0 is deliberately not design work — it is a week of measurable wins that depend on none of the decisions above, which means it can start tomorrow while the identity is still being argued about. Every phase ships to production.

Stop the bleeding

No design decisions required. Pure remediation of what the audit found, scriptable in an afternoon each.

  • Extract the base64 blobs from the three excursion pages. Athens is 13.5MB and Santorini 12.9MB — both are effectively unusable on cellular.
  • Batch-convert and resize all 258 raster images to AVIF with WebP fallback. The 4.3MB PNG photographs are the worst offenders.
  • Add width, height and lazy loading to all 768 image tags with a script.
  • Change two hex values to fix the tan contrast failure. One-line commit, real accessibility win.
  • Preload the hero and move it out of a CSS background.

Before Phase 1

Four things I assumed

Everything above is grounded in what is actually in the repository. These four are not, and each would change the work.

Highest impact

The competitor list is inferred

I built the three tiers from your FAQ, which names Amex FHR and Chase directly. You asked about competitors you want to outperform — if you have specific advisors or brands in mind, that changes the positioning section and possibly the visual bar.

Strategic

Is the retainer the priority?

It is your highest-margin product and it is currently one paragraph on the Services page, by invitation. If it is the growth engine, the Habitual segment moves from Phase 6 to Phase 2 and the whole IA shifts.

Blocking for identity

Photography rights

The recommendation to lead with your own frames assumes hotel-supplied imagery is licensed to you with restrictions. If you have broad rights and a large personal library, the rule stands anyway on strategy — but the transition is much cheaper.

Phase 4 is written to land with the next Collection. If that date is fixed and close, Phase 4 moves ahead of Phase 3 and the homepage waits.
