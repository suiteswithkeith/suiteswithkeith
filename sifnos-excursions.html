The Shape Of It

The island, and where everything sits

No airport, one port, and a mountain spine with the villages strung along the top. Ferries dock at Kamares in the west; Apollonia, Artemonas and Kastro sit in the middle and the east; the beaches fan out to the south. Tap any pin for what it is.

Pins mark the general area. I send exact directions with your itinerary.

Arriving by ferry

Everything comes in at Kamares. Roughly two and a half hours on the fast boat from Piraeus, or under an hour from Milos and Serifos. There is no airport, which is a big part of why it stays the way it is.

A car, for most days

Roads are good and short. A small car or a driver for the stay makes the beaches and the dinners easy; taxis exist but there are only a handful on the island.

When to come

Late May through June, and September into early October. Warm water, open tavernas, and none of the August crush on the Kastro lanes.

Bookable · One

Private experiences

These are the days I arrange myself, privately for the two of you or your group. Sifnos is the island of Tselementes, the father of the modern Greek cookbook, and it has been turning clay since antiquity, so the classes here are the real thing rather than a show put on for visitors.

Half day · Private

Cooking class in a village kitchen

A morning in a Sifnian home kitchen with a local cook, making the dishes the island is known for: revithada slow-baked in a clay pot, mastelo, and the little pastries for after. Lunch is what you made, with local wine at the table.

If you only book one thing on Sifnos, make it this.

Half day · Private

Pottery with a master potter

Sifnos has been making pottery for three thousand years and a handful of family workshops still fire in wood kilns by the sea. Time at the wheel with the potter, then your piece glazed, fired and shipped home.

Half day · Private

A farm morning

Out to a working farm in the hills for the cheese, honey, capers and thyme Sifnos cooks with. Walk the terraces with the family that runs it, then a long table under the trees with what they make.

Half or full day · Private

A guided hike

The island has some of the best waymarked footpaths in the Cyclades, past dovecotes, chapels and ancient watchtowers. I match the route to how hard you want to work, from the easy coastal walk to Chrysopigi to the climb up to the monastery on Profitis Ilias, and end it at a taverna.

Bookable · Two

The beaches

Sifnos beaches are small, sheltered and mostly sandy, with a taverna on almost every one. Choose by mood: a long lazy day on the sand, or a swim off the rocks below a village.

South · Sandy · Tavernas

Platis Gialos

The long, easy one. A wide sweep of sand with Omega3, Maiòlica and To Steki along it, so lunch is a few steps from the towel.

Southwest · Calm bay

Vathi

A near-enclosed bay with water like a lake and the little Taxiarchis chapel at the water's edge. Tables in the sand at Tsikali for lunch, and the best place on the island to do nothing.

Southeast · Iconic

Chrysopigi and Apokofto

The whitewashed monastery out on its rock, and the small sandy cove right beside it. Swim at Apokofto, walk out to the monastery, and lunch at the taverna above the beach.

Southeast · Small

Faros and Fasolou

A tiny fishing harbor with two little beaches a short walk apart. Fasolou is the quieter one. Pelicanos sits right on the sand at Faros.

East · Off the rocks

Seralia and Poulati

No sand, just deep blue water. Seralia is the old harbor below Kastro; Poulati is a ladder into the sea under a cliff-top monastery. Go late afternoon, then walk up to Loggia for sunset.

North · End of the road

Cheronissos and Vroulidia

Cheronissos is a fishing hamlet at the very top of the island, with pottery workshops and a taverna on the bay. Vroulidia, on the way, is a pebble cove with the clearest water on Sifnos.

The Tables

Where I would eat

Sifnos has a better table-to-acre ratio than anywhere in the Cyclades. These are the ones I send everyone to.

The two I book first

Platis Gialos · Best fish

Omega3

A tiny beachside fish bar with an outsized reputation: ceviche, sashimi and inventive seafood small plates with a Greek-only wine list. Best fish on the island. Reserve ahead; lunch is lovely.

Kastro · Best sunset

Loggia Wine Bar

A clifftop natural wine bar above the Church of the Seven Martyrs, with 100+ small-producer Greek wines, cheese boards and vinyl playing. The golden-hour tables are the most coveted seats on Sifnos, so arrive before 7pm.

Pelicanos

Cantina's laid-back sister restaurant: tables in the sand, great cocktails and the same seasonal ethos. The perfect swim, then lunch, then linger day. Also books via WhatsApp.

Classic tavernas

Vathi Beach · Since 1983

Tsikali

A family-run taverna with tables in the sand under tamarisk trees. They raise their own meat and make their own Sifnian goat cheese. Order the mastelo, the wine-braised goat.

Website →
Platis Gialos · No reservation

To Steki

The classic beachfront taverna: grilled octopus, fried calamari and revithada, Sifnos' famous chickpea soup, right on the sand. Easy, and no reservation needed.

TripAdvisor →
Apollonia · Since 1950

Drakakis

A historic kafeneio on Apollonia's main lane: Greek coffee, homemade sweets and meze at an old-world pace. Great for an evening stroll through town when the alleys are buzzing.
