Plan my Lake Como stay

The first Lake Como decision isn’t which hotel. It’s which part of the lake.

Near the town of Como — Cernobbio, Moltrasio and Torno — you’re about an hour from Milan, with quieter villages and easy arrivals. In the middle of the lake — Tremezzo, Cadenabbia and Bellagio — you’re where the lake is at its most beautiful, with the villages and villas a short boat ride away, but the road in is slow.

I stayed at Il Sereno, Villa Serbelloni and the EDITION, and toured Grand Hotel Tremezzo, Passalacqua and Villa d’Este. Booking through me costs the same as booking direct, and my clients typically get a space-available upgrade, daily breakfast and a hotel credit on top — plus the cars and private boats that make Como easy.

Hotel by hotel

Where I’d stay on Lake Como

In the order I ranked them — and every one is right for someone.

Mid-lake · Toured, #01 in my ranking

Grand Hotel Tremezzo

Art Nouveau grand hotel

Like stepping into a Wes Anderson film: tiered gardens with my favorite pool on the property, five restaurants, a clay court and lake-view junior suites facing Bellagio. It’s lively, and outside guests come in numbers.

Right for
Families, first trips to the lake, couples who like a buzz
Room I’d book
Book a junior suite or above.Garden-view rooms look out on pretty gardens and cost less, but the stars are the junior suites and above with lake views and balconies. The hotel recently reworked its rooms to add more of them, which was a smart move.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

It’s busy. Outside guests are welcome and come in large numbers, and it’s a magnet for photos. If privacy matters most, look at Il Sereno or Passalacqua.

Great for families, but mind the stairs. It’s much better suited to families than the lake’s intimate villas, but the property is built into the hillside and there are a lot of stairs.

Service is relaxed. I had lunch at the restaurant closest to the lobby. Service was solid, neither overly formal nor overly attentive, which suits the hotel’s easygoing mood.

The verdict

Just a fun hotel. Book a lake-view junior suite, spend your afternoons at the garden pool, and know you’ll be sharing it with a crowd.

Near Como · Stayed, #02 in my ranking

Il Sereno

Modern lakefront design hotel

I couldn’t find a single fault. Patricia Urquiola’s design right on the water in Torno, a heated pool, a hot tub in the old boathouse and its own Rivas. Private, quiet and built for couples.

Right for
Couples, honeymoons, design lovers, anyone who wants privacy

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

The magic starts at the Grand Suite. Every room sees the lake, but entry rooms get a partial view, some through the trees. The Grand Suite Lago adds the head-on view and a private terrace, and it starts at about what an entry room costs at Passalacqua. The Garden Lake View Suite is larger but sees the lake at an angle — my Lake Como proposal breaks down both.

Modern is the point. If your Como dream is frescoes and history, that's Villa d'Este or Passalacqua — this is the lake for design lovers. Or have both: two nights at Passalacqua, then ten minutes across the water to here.

Couples, not families. It’s not a family hotel. I’d only send families with older teenagers who are happy by the pool. For a family trip on the lake, look at Grand Hotel Tremezzo or the Mandarin Oriental.

The verdict

The best true lakefront hotel on Lake Como. I stayed in October 2026 and couldn’t find a single fault. Book it if you want privacy, design and the water right outside your room.

Near Como · Toured, #03 in my ranking

Passalacqua

Historic villa showpiece (Fora Reserve)

A whimsical 18th-century villa that feels like your own home, with only 24 rooms and no outside guests. Not my personal style, but the best honeymoon hotel on the lake.

Right for
Honeymoons, milestones, two couples traveling together

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

It's small and books far ahead. Only a handful of suites — the sooner we hold dates, the better. This is the single hardest confirmation on the lake.

The Junior Suite Al Lago is the one. It opens right toward the water — the room I show in my Lake Como proposal for milestone trips. Rooms in the Palazzo and the lakeside rooms are generously sized; rooms elsewhere can be small, and because this is a terraced garden property, most rooms see the lake in the distance rather than up close.

Only guests get in. The restaurants and spa are closed to outside visitors, which is a big part of why it feels so private. If you’re staying nearby and want to see it, I can sometimes arrange a lunch or a tour for my clients.

The verdict

A villa you never want to leave. Book it for a milestone — worth every euro if the moment calls for it.

Near Como · Toured, #04 in my ranking

Villa d'Este

Grand renaissance estate (Fora Reserve)

The lake’s original grand hotel: polished, warm, livelier than its reputation, with eight clay courts and Cernobbio at the gate. A planned refresh should lift it further.

Right for
Classicists, tennis trips with friends, multigenerational groups

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Grand, but not stiff. I’m told they plan to reduce the number of rooms and modernize, which will put it in the same tier as Il Sereno, Passalacqua and Grand Hotel Tremezzo. If you’re choosing between Villa d’Este and Villa Serbelloni, I’d steer you here.

Cernobbio is a smart base. The town side of the lake — easy access to Como city and the villa circuit by boat.

Compare the lake's icons before you book. Villa d'Este, Passalacqua, the Mandarin, Il Sereno, and the EDITION each fit a different couple — my Lake Como proposal shows how I frame the choice.

The verdict

One of Lake Como’s grandest estates: beautiful gardens, eight clay courts and service that’s polished and warm.

Mid-lake · Stayed, #05 in my ranking

The Lake Como EDITION

Modern design hotel in a converted lakefront building (EDITION)

A lively 148-room EDITION next door to Grand Hotel Tremezzo, with an outstanding spa and gym. Book a lake view on the third floor or above.

Right for
Younger travelers, LGBTQ+ travelers, families in the two-bedroom suite, fans of lively modern hotels
Room I’d book
Book a lake view, on the third floor or above.Below the third floor, lake-view rooms sit right above the casual restaurant, which is loud into the evening. Non-lake-view rooms face either the spa across the street or an interior courtyard, and they’re likely dark; unlike at Villa d’Este, Passalacqua or Grand Hotel Tremezzo, there’s no pretty garden view to fall back on.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Don’t count on a view upgrade. The hotel told me directly that a non-lake-view booking won’t be upgraded to a lake view; that takes a paid supplement. A standard lake-view booking can be upgraded to a lake-view loft or suite, so it’s the better starting point.

Dining is limited. There are three or four outlets, all on the expensive side, and the casual and in-room menus are short; there’s no pizza, for example. Grand Hotel Tremezzo is a short walk away for meals, and a boat opens up Bellagio, Varenna and the smaller restaurants I like on the lake, such as Silvio, Crotto del Misto and Locanda La Tirlindana.

There’s no town at the door. There isn’t much within walking distance. If you want a town right outside, Villa d’Este in Cernobbio is the better choice. Bellagio is a short boat ride away, but it’s very crowded, even in October.

The verdict

A neat, modern hotel that isn’t for everyone, but is right for a lot of travelers. Book a lake-view room on the third floor or above. If you’d rather have a classic grand hotel at a similar price, Grand Hotel Tremezzo next door is my pick.

Bellagio · Stayed, #06 in my ranking

Grand Hotel Villa Serbelloni

Historic grand hotel

Very historic, with a stunning modern spa and the best lake swimming on Como. The decor is dated, and Bellagio is very crowded.

Right for
Lovers of historic Italian hotels and art, swimmers, travelers set on Bellagio

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Bellagio is crowded. Packed with day-trippers, even in October; July and August would be a nightmare. You can visit Bellagio easily by boat from almost anywhere on the lake, so think hard about whether you want to sleep there.

The decor is very traditional. Frescoes, chandeliers, damask and antiques, all beautifully kept. If you love that, you’ll love it here. If you prefer something fresher, look elsewhere.

A big hotel with the village at the door. More than 90 rooms and three places to eat on property, plus all of Bellagio’s restaurants outside the front door.

The verdict

A beautiful, very historic hotel with a stunning spa and the lake’s best swimming. The dated decor and Bellagio’s crowds are why I usually steer clients to Villa d’Este.

Know before you go

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

01
Arrive by boat for the middle of the lake. The road from the town of Como to Tremezzo, Cadenabbia or Bellagio is narrow and takes 45 minutes to an hour. I arrange a private car from Milan to the water in Como, then about 30 minutes by private boat to your hotel’s dock. For Passalacqua, Villa d’Este, the Mandarin Oriental and Il Sereno, a car from Milan, about an hour, is simpler.
02
See Bellagio, but think twice about sleeping there. It’s the lake’s most famous village and very crowded, even in October. Visit by boat and come home somewhere calmer.
03
Book boats through me. Private boats for dinners across the lake, a day on the water or the move between hotels are the best part of Como. I work with the lake’s best captains, at fair prices.
04
Mind the season. Most of the lake’s luxury hotels close for winter. Il Sereno is open mid-March to October 26; Passalacqua stays open through New Year’s. May, June and September are the sweet spot.
05
Floating pools rock. Several hotels have pools floating on the lake, which move with the water. If that bothers you, Il Sereno’s heated pool is on land, and Grand Hotel Tremezzo’s garden pool sits above the lake.
Make it one trip

Two hotels, one lake.

For a honeymoon, I’d split the stay: two or three nights at Passalacqua, then a ten-minute private boat across the lake to Il Sereno. Two completely different stays, and only one travel day.
