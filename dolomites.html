The first decision

Where to base yourself

The Dolomites aren’t one place. Each base suits a different trip.

Val Gardena & Ortisei
The best all-round base. A real town with great restaurants, and lifts straight to Seceda’s jagged ridgeline and to the Alpe di Siusi meadows. Busy in July and August. Stay at Gardena Grödnerhof.
The Alpe di Siusi
Europe’s largest high-alpine meadow, ringed by the Sassolungo and the Sciliar, just over the ridge from Ortisei. A protected area with restricted car access, trails and slopes from your door, and the best skiing location. Stay at COMO Alpina.
Above Bressanone
A quiet mountainside facing the Odle, about 25 minutes above the town of Bressanone (Brixen). Come to slow down. It isn’t a place to hike from the door. Stay at Forestis.
Alta Badia & San Cassiano
A village in a fantastic spot for skiing and hiking, quiet in the shoulder seasons and buzzing in winter and summer. Stay at Aman Rosa Alpina.
Merano
Technically outside the main Dolomites valleys: a spa town with a gentler climate and big views. An easy first or last stop. Stay at Miramonti.
Hotel by hotel

Where I’d stay in the Dolomites

My favorite first, and every one is right for someone.

Above Bressanone · Stayed, my favorite

Forestis

Alpine wellness retreat

The best view of the Odle of any luxury hotel in the region, from your room, the restaurant, the pools and the spa. Ask for a high floor in the tower closest to the lodge. Adults 14 and over in the hotel; families book the five-bedroom Forestis Villa.

Right for
Honeymoons, couples, wellness, total disconnection

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Your floor and tower matter, and only a request secures them. There’s no separate room category for higher floors or a particular tower; a tower suite can be on any floor of any of the three towers. The best views of the Odle are from the fourth or fifth floor and above, in the tower closest to the historic lodge and in the middle tower, where you’re above the tree line. Securing one of those is the real upgrade here, and it’s exactly what I push for with the team for my clients.

The lodge suites are good value. The suites in the historic lodge are spacious and well priced. If the tower suites are beyond the budget, I’d happily book one.

Expect German and Austrian sauna culture. The main sauna area is mixed. You walk around in a towel or robe, but it comes off in the saunas and steam room, which is normal in this corner of Italy. Your body isn’t supposed to touch the wood, so you sit or lie on your towel in the saunas, and on a small towel in the steam room. There’s also a swimsuit sauna if you prefer.

The verdict

For peace and quiet, wellness, great food and awe-inspiring views, there’s nowhere better in the Dolomites.

Ortisei · Stayed, a close second

Gardena Grödnerhof

Renovated alpine hotel in town

An incredible spa, a great gym and service to match. Book a Superior Deluxe or above, and come outside of July and August. Not ski-in, ski-out, but the lifts are close.

Right for
Couples, families, hikers, foodies

Alpe di Siusi · Stayed

Mountain wellness hotel

Walk out of your room onto the trails or the slopes. Not quite the hotel Forestis, Gardena or Aman is, but the price reflects that, and the spa and pool hold their own. Mind the chairlift outside the Mountain View rooms.

Right for
Skiers, hikers, wellness

The seasons

When to go

01
Summer, late June to mid-September. Hiking season: every lift is running and the days are long, but Ortisei, Seceda and the Alpe di Siusi are at their busiest. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in July and August, so start hikes early.
02
September and early October. My favorite. Quieter trails, crisp clear days and, by mid-October, golden larches. Lifts start closing in October, and villages like San Cassiano go quiet.
03
Winter, December to March. Ski season, on the Dolomiti Superski network that links the valleys. COMO Alpina is the only true ski-in, ski-out hotel on my list; Aman Rosa Alpina is a two-minute shuttle from the lifts.
04
In between. Many hotels and lifts close between seasons, roughly April to May and from late October or November into December. Check before you set dates; I do this for every trip.
Days out

What to do

The two big lift-served landscapes are completely different, and both start in Ortisei.

01
Seceda. The postcard ridgeline above Val Gardena, by lift from Ortisei. The lift is busy and needs a time-slotted ticket bought in advance. The Col Raiser lift from Santa Cristina, about 10 minutes from Ortisei, reaches the same area with some planning.
02
The Alpe di Siusi. Rolling meadows under the Sassolungo and the Sciliar, by gondola from Ortisei. A completely different landscape from Seceda, and easy walking for all levels.
03
A private guide. I’d always choose one over going with the crowds. They know when and where to go, and the good ones book up in summer, so I arrange them ahead.
04
A heli-hike. If the budget allows, a helicopter up to the high ground and a guided hike down is the most memorable day you can have here. I arrange it.
05
The spas. Every hotel on my list has an excellent one, and in this part of Italy they’re a reason to come in their own right.
Arriving

Getting there & getting around

The Dolomites take more planning than most of Italy. I handle all of it.

01
Flying in. Innsbruck is the closest major airport, about two hours from Ortisei or Forestis. Verona is about two and a half hours, and Venice, Munich and Milan are about three to four, depending on the hotel and traffic.
02
By train. The main line from Verona runs up through Bolzano and Bressanone to Innsbruck and Munich. Get off at Bolzano for Ortisei, the Alpe di Siusi or Merano, or at Bressanone for Forestis, and finish by private car.
03
Private drivers beat self-driving. Mountain passes are narrow and winding, parking in Ortisei is tight in summer, and the road up the Alpe di Siusi is restricted to permit holders. A private driver takes all of that off your plate.
04
By helicopter. A helicopter transfer from Lake Como, Venice or Milan saves hours, weather permitting.
05
Between hotels. Most pairings are about an hour by car: Ortisei to Forestis, Ortisei to San Cassiano over the Gardena Pass, and Ortisei to Merano. COMO Alpina is just over the mountain from Ortisei.
Know before you go

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

01
Know the sauna rules. The main sauna areas are mixed, men and women together. You’ll walk around the spa in a towel or robe, but swimsuits and wraps come off in the saunas and steam rooms. Your body isn’t supposed to touch the wood, so sit or lie on your towel. It’s very German and Austrian, and it applies at every hotel on my list.
02
Pack layers, whatever the month. Mountain weather changes fast, and it can be much colder at the top of a lift than in the valley.
03
Avoid Ortisei in peak summer if you want quiet. It’s a wonderful town, but in July and August the crowds follow you onto the trails.
04
Book the busy lifts and the guides ahead. Seceda’s time slots and the best mountain guides go early in summer.
05
Split the trip. Two or three nights each at two very different hotels is the best way to see the region. My favorite pairings are Gardena Grödnerhof with Forestis, and COMO Alpina with Gardena Grödnerhof. Add Venice at either end: Airelles Palladio pairs naturally with any of my Dolomites hotels, about two and a half to three hours away by private car.
