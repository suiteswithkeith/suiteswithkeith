hotel by hotel

Restored medieval borghi, hilltop castles and a 5,000-acre wine estate — the Tuscany hotels I recommend, and which one is right for your trip.

Plan my Tuscany stay

In Tuscany the hotel is the trip. Pick the right one and you’ll barely want to leave it.

The countryside stays here fall into two families. The borghi are whole medieval villages restored and run as one hotel — you sleep inside the village. The castles and estates are grander, with the vineyards, golf and Michelin dining to fill a week.

I’ve stayed at Borgo San Felice and Borgo Pignano and toured Il Borro. All three are Fora Reserve, and all three are fantastic — for three different travelers. That’s the heart of this guide.

Know before you go

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

01
You’ll want a car (or a driver). Every hotel here is in the countryside. Pick up a car as you leave Florence, or let me arrange drivers who know the back roads — especially for wine-tasting days.
02
Match the estate to the traveler, not the photo. Honeymooners belong in Il Borro’s original borgo; families and groups in Pignano’s villas; almost anyone at San Felice. Tell me who’s coming and I’ll match you.
03
Three nights minimum. These are places to settle into. With fewer nights, the pool, the long dinners and the drives out all compete.
04
Think about where you’ll explore. San Felice and Casole put Siena close by; Castello del Nero sits between Florence and Siena; Pignano is near Volterra and San Gimignano; Castiglion del Bosco is deep in the Val d’Orcia.
05
Fora Reserve perks apply. Booking San Felice, Pignano or Il Borro through me adds a space-available upgrade, daily breakfast and a hotel credit, at the same rate as booking direct.
