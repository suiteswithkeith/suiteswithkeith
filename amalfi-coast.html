The Amalfi Coast,
hotel by hotel

Positano, Amalfi and the cliffs in between. Where to base yourself, who each hotel is for and which room to ask for, from my trip in October 2026.

Plan my Amalfi Coast stay

Start with where you want to wake up. The hotel follows.

Positano is the postcard, and it is busy. Just outside town, Il San Pietro and Villa Treville give you the quiet with a free shuttle in. Amalfi is slower, and Hotel Santa Caterina has been doing slow beautifully since 1904. Above Conca dei Marini, Borgo Santandrea is the modern statement, with a proper beach.

I’m a travel advisor, and in October 2026 I stayed at Le Sirenuse and Hotel Santa Caterina and toured Il San Pietro and Villa Treville. This is what I’d tell a friend.

Town by town

Where to base yourself

Positano
The postcard, and crazy-busy. Stay in the middle of it at Le Sirenuse, or on the cliffs just outside at Il San Pietro or Villa Treville, both with free shuttles into town.
Amalfi
Slower days, and a hotel that feels like a home. Hotel Santa Caterina has been run by the same family since 1904. Miramalfi, carved into the cliffside, has my #1 pool in the world.
Conca dei Marini
The modern statement, with a real beach. Just along the coast from Amalfi, Borgo Santandrea feels different from every other hotel on the coast.
Ravello
High above the sea, and quieter than Positano. Belmond Hotel Caruso has the most dramatic infinity pool on the coast, and the nearest helipad is in Scala, close by.

Hotel by hotel

Where I would stay on the Amalfi Coast

Three in Positano, two along the coast toward Amalfi.

The classic · Positano, just outside town

Il San Pietro

Sea views, and the sunset

The rooms are impeccably kept, and every one has a sea view: nautical, bright, blues and whites and tiles. Some face the sunrise, others the sunset, and the beach club is on the sunset side, which is a huge selling point. It sits right outside crazy-busy Positano, with free boat and car shuttles into town.

Right for
Honeymoons, tennis travelers, fall trips, anyone who wants privacy
Room I’d book
Pick your side.Tell me sunrise or sunset. Base rooms are fantastic; Signature and Prestige give you more space.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

The sunset beach club. In the fall, most of the coast loses the sun on the water around 4:30 or 5pm. Not here.

The tennis court. Iconic, and #1 in my hotel tennis court ranking.

Privacy. No one can look down on you, and you will only see other guests.

The inclusions. A complimentary 2-hour yacht cruise, cooking and pizza classes, a daily cocktail masterclass, Pilates and yoga.

The verdict

The classic, done properly. One of the two best options in Positano.

The private one · Positano, just outside town

Villa Treville

A private estate on the cliffs

Villa Treville has probably the most private rooms in all of Positano. Every room is very different, not just a little different, and the artwork is cool and eclectic, but it really works. Service is warm and the team is outstanding.

Right for
Couples, honeymoons, anyone who wants Positano without the crowds
Room I’d book
Let me match you.Every room is different. The suite with the outdoor tub and shower is a standout.

Keith’s notes

Three boats. Three stunning boats, and free shuttles into Positano.

The beach club. On the rocks, right on the water.

The showers. One of the coolest I have ever seen: rain falling from an old brick dome.

A spa and gym with a view. An iron-and-glass garden pavilion, and a gym looking straight out to sea.

The verdict

Alongside Il San Pietro, the best option in Positano, and maybe slightly better value.

In town · The middle of Positano

Le Sirenuse

The scene

A really cool, lively property in the middle of Positano. That is either a selling point or a sore point, depending on who you are. Service is very formal, and you come to see and be seen. The art is stunning, the lookout over the dome is truly stunning, and the breakfast is out of this world.

Right for
Travelers who want to walk into town
Room I’d book
A true sea-view room or suite.The rest face the courtyard, or sit right on top of the pool, which is loud.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

The new beach club. Complimentary boats at 10:30 and 12:30.

The sunset cruise. Book it in advance. I learned that the hard way.

The breakfast. Incredible. Out of this world.

The rooftop pool. #02 on my worldwide pools list.

The verdict

If you want to walk into town, this is the one. Just book a true sea-view room.

The family hotel · Amalfi

Hotel Santa Caterina

A guest in someone’s home

Opened in 1904 by the town doctor of Amalfi, and still run by his granddaughters, Giusi and Carmela Gambardella. So many of the team have been here 15 to 20 years or more. The luxury is quiet and lives in the tiny details. I stayed in October 2026, and I will be back for five nights at least.

Right for
Slow stays, multigenerational trips, anyone who values real human service
Room I’d book
A front sea view.The Special Suite, with its own pool over the sea, for a milestone. Or ask me for room 45, where I stayed.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

The gardens. Take the stairs down to the beach club at least once. It feels like a fairy tale.

The elevator. The coolest view you have ever seen, on the way down to the beach.

Dial 260. No WhatsApp. Reception still keeps the calendar in real books with real pens.

Casa Marchesa. Twelve more modern, private rooms 300 meters away, with their own pool.

The verdict

For warmth and a hotel that knows exactly what it is doing. Stay five nights, then add a few in Positano.

The statement · Conca dei Marini

Borgo Santandrea

Spectacular, almost awe-inspiring

Above Conca dei Marini, just along the coast from Amalfi. It is very modern, in the rooms and in the facilities, and that makes it feel different from every other hotel on the Amalfi Coast. I liked that. The service is refined but warm.

Right for
Travelers who want a modern, spectacular statement and a real beach
Know before you book
The private pool rooms are not that private.You can look down on your neighbors, and they can look down on you. Not everyone will mind, but it is important to know.

Keith’s notes

The beach. Beautiful, massive and a proper beach.

Alici. Fantastic dining.

The service. Refined but warm.

The sun. It leaves the water early. In the fall, your swim time ends around 4:30 or 5pm.

When to go. May to June, or September.

The verdict

The modern, spectacular statement on the coast. Just think twice before booking a room with a private pool.

Know before you go

The fine print, from your advisor

01

02

Before you ask

Amalfi Coast FAQ

Where should I stay on the Amalfi Coast?

In Positano, Il San Pietro or Villa Treville, both on the cliffs just outside town, or Le Sirenuse if you want to walk into town. In Amalfi, Hotel Santa Caterina. For a modern statement with a proper beach, Borgo Santandrea above Conca dei Marini.

Should I stay in Positano or Amalfi?

Many of my clients do both: Hotel Santa Caterina for slow days in Amalfi, then Il San Pietro or Villa Treville for a few nights in Positano. I can split the stay.

Where should I stay in Positano?

For me, the two best options are Il San Pietro and Villa Treville, both on the cliffs just outside town. If you want to walk into town, pick Le Sirenuse. See the three side by side.

Is it better to stay in Positano or just outside it?

Just outside, for most of my clients. Positano is busy. Il San Pietro and Villa Treville both run free shuttles into town, so you get the quiet without giving up the town.

Which Amalfi Coast hotel has the best beach club?

Il San Pietro, because it is on the sunset side. In the fall, most beach clubs on the coast lose the sun around 4:30 or 5pm. Il San Pietro keeps it. For a big, proper beach, Borgo Santandrea.

Which is the most private?

Villa Treville. It has probably the most private rooms in Positano, with Il San Pietro close behind.

Santa Caterina or Borgo Santandrea?

Borgo Santandrea is spectacular and brand new in feel. Santa Caterina is the opposite: it has been here for over a century and it knows exactly what it is doing. If you want warmth and history over a statement, choose Santa Caterina.

How do I get to the Amalfi Coast?

By boat from Naples or Salerno, which is practical and enjoyable for Il San Pietro, Villa Treville, Santa Caterina and Borgo Santandrea, or by private car. The nearest helipad is in Scala, close to Ravello. I arrange all of it.
