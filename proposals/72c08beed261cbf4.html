Step one · Pick your route

Paris, the Riviera, Lake Como, then Greece

Every version starts the same way: Paris for 3 nights, the Riviera for 3, and Lake Como for at least 3. The question is what you do between the lake and the islands.

Venice for 2 nights (see Venice). My recommendation. A fast train from the lake, then a short direct flight from Venice to Athens. Then 5 nights in Greece.

Milan for 2 nights (see Milan). An hour from the lake, with the Duomo, the Last Supper and the best shopping in Italy. Then 5 nights in Greece.

Straight to Greece. A fourth night on Lake Como and a sixth night on the island. The most relaxed version, with only four hotels.

Why northern Italy, and no Rome

Rome deserves at least three or four nights, it's hot and crowded in mid-June, and it pulls the whole route south and back again. Staying in the north keeps every leg short: a drive along the coast from the Riviera to the lake, a train to Venice or Milan, and a direct flight to Athens. You spend your days on the water, not in transit.

Why I'd add Venice

Venice is unlike anywhere else on earth, and it's the stop a 17-year-old is most likely to remember. On these dates you'd be there on a Monday and Tuesday, when the hotels cost less than on a weekend. Milan is a great city too, but after the Riviera and the lake, Venice adds something completely different. If the family would rather slow down, skipping both is a lovely trip too.

Getting between stops

Short hops, no wasted days

I'll book every leg for you, with drivers waiting at each station, airport and dock so nobody is dragging four suitcases across Venice or up a Santorini hill.

Paris to the Riviera

The fast train from Gare de Lyon is about 5.5 hours to Antibes, and a lovely ride. The flight to Nice is about an hour and a half and saves most of a day. With only three nights in Paris, I lean toward flying.

The Riviera to Lake Como

A private driver along the coast past Monaco and Genoa, then up to the lake, about four and a half hours with a lunch stop. Or fly Nice to Milan in about an hour and drive the last hour to the lake.

Lake Como to Venice or Milan

Milan is about an hour by car or train. Venice is about three and a half hours by fast train through Milan, then a water taxi to your hotel. I'll book first class seats together.

On to the islands

Fly direct from Venice or Milan to Athens, about two and a half hours, then Athens to Santorini, about 45 minutes. As we discussed, that flight beats the helicopter by a mile: the helicopter runs about €6,000 for the four of you, and the flight costs a small fraction of that. Mykonos is a similar short flight, and Ios is a short ferry ride from Santorini. At the end, you fly back to Athens and home to Vancouver.
