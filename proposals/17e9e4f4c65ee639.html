How I’m thinking about it

Rome and Athens for the history, then six slow nights by the sea. Here are three ways to spend them, and where I’d put the money in each.

Six nights
Six nights covers one island properly plus a short Santorini finale, or one big island done slowly. Three islands in six nights would mean packing every other morning, so I’ve left that out.
Santorini
Two nights is the right dose. You get one sunset from your own terrace and one out on the water. It’s also where a splurge costs least overall, because you’re only paying for two nights.
Crete
Crete is huge, so splitting three or four nights there with Santorini means a lot of time in the car. Give it all six nights and it becomes one of the best options here: one hotel, no ferries, a 50-minute flight from Athens on your miles.
June
Mid-June is peak season. The water is warm and the days are long, but the best rooms go early, so the sooner we choose, the better the options.

Each one picks up the morning you leave Athens and ends with your flight home on June 20.

The questions you’ll have

Why not Crete and Santorini together?
It works, and the ferry is only about two hours. But with three or four nights on Crete, you’d spend much of that time in the car. If Crete is the draw, give it all six nights. If Santorini is, pair it with a smaller island.
Is two nights enough in Santorini?
For a finale, yes. You arrive by early afternoon, have a full day and two sunsets, and leave before the crowds wear on you. Most people who stay longer spend the extra days at the pool.
Which island would you choose?
For a honeymoon, Folegandros with Gundari. If the budget matters more, Sifnos at Verina Astra, then Canaves to finish.
What about Rome and Athens?
They’re next. Once you’ve picked an island route, I’ll send a short list of well-located, sensibly priced hotels in both cities so the whole trip flows.
