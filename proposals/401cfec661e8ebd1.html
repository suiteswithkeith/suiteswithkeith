Assyrtiko grown in low basket-coiled vines in volcanic ash — bone-dry, saline, unmistakable. Four wines and four bites, each pairing made for the glass. The easiest thing here to slot into an afternoon.

The old capital, stacked inside its castle walls on the island’s highest hill. Ten minutes of climbing lanes gets you a view over both coasts at once — and almost nobody is up there. My favorite contrarian sunset spot, too.

A Bronze Age town buried by the eruption and dug back out — three-story houses, paved streets, frescoes. Under a roof, which makes it the right call on a hot day. Addison’s history elective, live.

The Wildcard · An idea, not a change of plan

Or: open on Mykonos instead

Hear me out. Santorini is the postcard, and if it's the dream, we keep it. But if you're open to it, I think Mykonos might actually fit the two of you better for the opening act: the hotel options are stronger, it's more of a mother-daughter island — beach clubs, boutiques, real evenings out together — and in May it's none of the chaos it's famous for in August. It's so perfect that time of year. It's also, honestly, more culturally interesting: ancient Delos, one of Greece's great archaeological sites, is a morning's boat ride away. And the connections work identically — direct flights from Athens, and Paros or Milos are an easy hop onward.

Better hotels

Two of my Greece Top 10 and my Best New Resort in Greece are on this island. Santorini's suites are beautiful; Mykonos' resorts are complete.

May is the secret

The famous crowds and the meltemi wind are July–August problems. In May you get the beach clubs, the water and the town at half volume and full charm.

More to actually do

Delos and Rhenia by boat, a day trip to the villages of Tinos, beach clubs from bohemian to glamorous, and Mykonos Town's whitewashed maze after dark.

Same easy logistics

Fly Athens → Mykonos in 40 minutes, then a short ferry on to Paros or Milos on May 22. Nothing else about the trip changes.

The Island Guide · If you pick Paros

Five nights of villages, beaches & slow days

Paros is the island I recommend more every year — big enough for distinct villages and beaches, compact enough that everything is within reach. It’s the one island where I say rent a small car for the whole stay: nothing is more than forty minutes from anything else, so the hill villages, the far beaches, and dinner wherever you want it all become your neighborhood.

Beyond the boat day
Evening · 3.5 hours
Evening sail & dinner on deck

Sails up at six from Piso Livadi, a drink in hand, a swim in a sheltered bay, then dinner served on deck as the sun drops. The gentler sister to the full Antiparos day — lovely on its own night.

From $110 per person
Half day · No booking
Antiparos, the easy way

The ten-minute car ferry from Pounda runs every half hour: one long main street of galleries and good tavernas, the cave in the hills above, and Soros beach on the way back. A different island for the cost of a coffee.

The Island Guide · If you pick Milos

Five nights on nature’s masterpiece

Milos feels unlike anywhere else in the Cyclades — every beach looks different, every coastline surprises you, and the landscape shifts from volcanic cliffs to white rock to hidden coves. A car (or an ATV, if Addison talks you into it) is essential: the best of the island sits at the end of short dirt tracks, and the freedom to explore at your own pace is the experience. For boat days I work with Salty Rides, my preferred partner on Milos.
