The shortlist

The 10 hotels in Italy I’d send my own clients to

Ranked, with the room I’d ask for at each. That last part is where booking through me pays off.

01

Airelles Palladio · Venice

My number one hotel in Italy.

The most beautiful hotel I’ve seen in Italy, on Giudecca: gardens, a 25-meter pool, a three-story spa, four restaurants and a kids club, five minutes by boat from St. Mark’s. Three nights is my Venice sweet spot. Here I could easily do four.

The room I’d ask for. The Suite del Doge for the big occasion, or Villa Mason, the three-bedroom suite with its own pool, for a family. Booked through me, stays of two nights or more include the water taxi from the airport.

Read my review →
02

Hotel Santa Caterina · Amalfi

The warmest welcome on the coast.

A family hotel since 1904, where many of the team have been for 15 to 20 years. Walk down through the gardens to the beach club, ride the elevator back up, and stay at least five nights. This is a hotel to slow down in.

The room I’d ask for. A front sea view: a Special Suite with its own pool, or room 45, where I stayed.

Read my review →
03

Hotel Eden · Rome

One of my top three stays in all of Italy.

Anticipatory, warm service, bright rooms, a fantastic restaurant upstairs, and a quiet residential street just above the Spanish Steps. It usually costs a little less than the Hassler, the de Russie and the Bulgari, for a stay I’d rank above all three.

The room I’d ask for. The top suites, which can be connected for families or two couples traveling together. I’ll map out what’s possible.

Read my review →
04

Forestis · The Dolomites

My favorite hotel in the Dolomites.

High above Bressanone, with the best view of the Odle of any luxury hotel in the region. It won my categories for wellness, views, dining, service and privacy. Adults 14 and over; families can book the five-bedroom Forestis Villa.

The room I’d ask for. A tower suite on the fourth or fifth floor or above, in the tower closest to the historic lodge or the middle tower, where you’re above the tree line. There’s no room category for it. Only a request secures it, and I push for it.

Read my review →
05

Grand Hotel Tremezzo · Lake Como

My favorite hotel on the lake.

The 1910 landmark in the middle of the lake, looking straight across at Bellagio, with five restaurants and the famous floating pool. It’s the most fun hotel on Como, and my pick for families.

The room I’d ask for. A junior suite or above, for the lake view and a balcony. Garden-view rooms cost less, but the lake rooms are the stars.

Read my review →
06

Il Sereno · Lake Como

The best true lakefront hotel on Como.

Modern, glass-walled and right on the water in Torno, and every room faces the lake. It’s built for couples, and it’s ten minutes by private boat from Passalacqua. Two nights at each is the Como honeymoon I’d book.

The room I’d ask for. The Grand Suite Lago, for the head-on lake view and a private terrace. It starts at about what an entry room costs at Passalacqua.

Read my review →
07

Il San Pietro di Positano · Positano

The beach club that keeps the sun.

On the cliffs just outside Positano, with a free shuttle into town. In the fall, most beach clubs on the coast lose the sun around 4:30 or 5pm. Il San Pietro is on the sunset side, so it doesn’t.

The room I’d ask for. Every room has a sea view, but some face the sunrise and others the sunset. If sunsets matter to you, I request the sunset side.

Read my review →
08

Borgo San Felice · Tuscany

The most well-rounded borgo in Tuscany.

A perfectly restored medieval hamlet in the Chianti hills, with fantastic service and food, tennis, a gym and a spa. One of my favorite stays of the whole trip. Give it at least three nights.

My tip. In summer, tables go out in the piazza in front of the chapel, with live music as the sun goes down. Tell me your dates and I’ll make sure you get one of those nights.

Read my review →
09

Passalacqua · Lake Como

My Lake Como honeymoon pick.

A 1787 villa in Moltrasio with just 24 rooms, and only hotel guests can use the restaurants and spa, so it feels like the place is yours. It’s also the hardest confirmation on the lake, so we hold dates early.

The room I’d ask for. The Junior Suite Al Lago, which opens right toward the water. Because Passalacqua is a terraced garden estate, most rooms see the lake in the distance.

Read my review →
10

Belmond Villa San Michele · Florence

The best views in Florence.

A renovated 15th-century monastery up in Fiesole, looking out over the city to the Duomo. The shuttle gets you into Florence in minutes, and it’s the romantic chapter I build into a Florence trip.

The room I’d ask for. A Garden Junior Suite above the main lawn. They’re more private than the ones below it, where people can look down on you.

Read my review →
Bonus

Grand Hotel Miramare · Santa Margherita Ligure

The real Italian Riviera, for about half the price.

A restored grand hotel in a real Riviera town, with a big pool, a spa and one of the largest beach clubs in Liguria. Sea-view rooms run around $1,000 to $1,500 a night, roughly half the Splendido next door in Portofino.

The room I’d ask for. A sea view on a lower floor, where the noble families once lived: more space and big terraces. The floor matters more than the room name.
