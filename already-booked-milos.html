No. 01

Your day on the water

Kleftiko is the reason you came

Booked · the first thing I hold on Milos

Volcanic rock and impossible water — Milos looks like nowhere else in Greece, and it’s best seen from a boat. Kleftiko’s sculpted white cliffs, the sea caves, and the swim stops along the western coastline that no road reaches: this is the day the whole trip is built around, and I charter it privately with Salty Rides, my preferred boat partner on the island, rather than through a booking site.

Kleftiko. The island’s most unforgettable day on the water — white cliffs carved into arches and caves, and water so clear the boat looks like it’s floating on air. Only reachable by sea.

The western coastline. Skinopi and Erēma are perfectly positioned for it: book the boat for the next morning and see the western coast before the crowds arrive.

Sarakiniko first. On the boat day I’d set out early for Sarakiniko’s lunar landscape before boarding — it’s the encore, and it’s empty at sunrise and full by ten.

Private over shared, always. A private boat is the day at your pace — the swim stops, the lunch, and the light. I’ll match the boat to your budget rather than the other way around.

The wind decides. The Meltemi can move a boat day with little notice, so I place it early in your stay with room to shift it — which you can’t do once you’ve prepaid a fixed date online.

If seeing an island from the water is the reason you booked Milos, this is the booking that has to happen first. Send me your dates and I’ll hold the boat before the good mornings are gone.

No. 02

Dinner reservations

A handful of tables, and a village to walk to

Booked · 2–4 weeks ahead in high season

Milos eats simpler than Paros — grilled fish, a table at the water’s edge, a village to wander afterward — and that’s the point. But the handful of places worth planning an evening around are small, and in July and August the waterfront tables at the hour worth having go to the people who booked. Tell me your dates and I’ll hold the nights that matter and leave the rest loose.

Medusa, Mandrakia. Grilled fish above a tiny fishing harbor — the first dinner I’d book on the island, and one of the two tables I hold before you land.

Armenaki, Pollonia. The other one. The village’s best seafood table, and if you’re at White Pebble it’s a stroll from your room — the village is the amenity.

O!Hamos, Adamas. The long farewell dinner overlooking the water — where the last night on Milos should end.

Lunch at Alkis, Pollonia. A relaxed waterfront lunch after a swim in Pollonia — the first afternoon of the trip, as I’d plan it.

Or stay in. Onero has a signature restaurant and private terrace dining; Skinopi gives you an outdoor kitchen and a breakfast basket on your terrace instead — on purpose. Some of the best evenings on Milos are the ones where you never leave.

Two tables held across a four-night stay, and let the rest be long lunches. Send me your dates and I’ll tell you which nights are already tight and what I can still get.

No. 03

Beaches & the car

Rent the car. Then leave the plan loose.

Best for · beach lovers, adventure travelers, anyone who’d rather find the cove themselves

Every beach on Milos looks different — volcanic cliffs, white rock, hidden coves — and the best of them are spread across the coastline at the end of dirt tracks. A car or ATV isn’t optional here; it’s the trip. I’ll have it waiting when you land. What you do with it is the part worth leaving unplanned.

Sarakiniko at sunrise. The iconic lunar landscape before the crowds arrive. Go once at dawn and you’ll understand why every photo of Milos is this beach.

The western beaches by ATV. Remote coves and dramatic coastline that are difficult to reach any other way — the half of the island most visitors never see.

A swim in Pollonia. The easy afternoon: swim, a waterfront lunch at Alkis, a wander through the village, and sunset cocktails overlooking the Aegean.

Kimolos, for a day or a night. The ferry leaves from Pollonia, and it’s one of the easiest day trips in the Cyclades — stay in a traditional boat house if you want one of the most authentic nights in the islands.

Milos rewards curiosity. Leave time to turn down roads you hadn’t planned to take — you’ll often discover your favorite beach by accident. I’ll book what needs booking and leave the afternoons alone.

The villages evening

Plaka, Klima, and dinner on the water

Milos’ villages are small, close together, and at their best in the last two hours of light. This is the evening I’d build once — in the right order, with the table held.

01Plaka, late afternoon. The hilltop village — local boutiques, authentic tavernas, and the island’s best sunset views. Wander the lanes before the light goes. Sunset from the hilltop · around 6:30pm
02Down to Klima. A few minutes below Plaka: the colorful fishermen’s houses at the water’s edge, and the postcard everyone comes for. If you’re at Skinopi it’s practically your neighborhood. Fishermen’s houses · golden hour
03Dinner overlooking the water. Medusa in Mandrakia or O!Hamos in Adamas — both a short drive, both tables I hold before you land. Booked ahead · around 9:00pm

Milos is small enough that this whole evening happens within twenty minutes of your hotel. I hold the table and put it on a night that doesn’t follow the boat day, so nobody is rushing back from the water to make it.

No. 04

The Athens add-on

Two or three nights, not a layover

Best for · bookending the trip, food lovers, first-time Greece

Most people treat Athens as a gateway to the islands. I think that’s a mistake. Give yourself two or three nights and you’ll discover one of Europe’s most exciting food cities, incredible luxury hotels, and neighborhoods that feel completely different from one another. It’s the perfect way to begin or end a trip through Greece.

Put it at the end, not the beginning. Flying home out of Athens gives you a buffer against a cancelled ferry or a delayed island flight — and you arrive in the city already unwound.

Two nights is the minimum that works. One night is an airport hotel with extra steps. Three is genuinely great.

Book the Acropolis early. Timed entry, and the first slot of the morning is the only one worth having in summer.

Rules I’d give any client

Never book the last ferry of the day. If it cancels, there’s nothing behind it.
Put the boat day early in the stay. If the wind moves it, you still have days to move it to.
Don’t fly home the same day you sail. Build in a night. The Aegean does not care about your itinerary.
Two islands per week. That’s the limit. Three islands in seven nights is three travel days.
