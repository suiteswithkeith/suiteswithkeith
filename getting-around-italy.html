Plan my Italy trip

The hotels are the easy part of Italy. The hours in between are where a trip goes right or wrong.

Rome to Florence is ninety minutes by train and three hours by road. Venice has no cars at all. The best hotels on Lake Como are reached by water, the Dolomites by mountain passes, and the Amalfi Coast by one famously narrow road. Every region moves differently, and a trip that treats them all the same loses half a day at every change.

This is how I move my clients: privately, by whatever is fastest and most comfortable for that leg, with every handover arranged before you land. You never carry a bag, read a departure board or wonder who is meeting you. Work with me and you get Fora perks at the hotels, typically a space-available upgrade, daily breakfast and a hotel credit, plus someone who knows these hotels personally. Same rate as booking direct.

The exception

When I book the train

I recommend the train in Italy for one reason only: on two legs it is faster and calmer than any car.

Rome ↔ Florence
About 1 hour 30 by fast train, against roughly three hours by road.
Florence ↔ Venice
A little over 2 hours by fast train, against roughly three hours by road, before the boat.
How it works
Never on your own. Your driver drops you at the station, where someone is waiting with your name on a sign. Porters take your bags to your seat in the top class. At the other end you’re met on the platform, and a car or water taxi is waiting outside. You carry nothing.
Everywhere else
Private. Lake Como, the Dolomites, Tuscany and the Amalfi Coast are all car, boat or helicopter trips. The train doesn’t reach the hotels, and the transfers at either end cancel out any time saved.
What it’s actually like

What I take off your plate

Italy has its quirks. These are the ones my clients never have to know about.

01
Restricted city centers. Florence, Rome and most hill towns have camera-enforced traffic zones. Your driver has the permits, or stops where you can walk in.
02
Venice has no cars. Bags, bridges and canals are a real puzzle with luggage. A water taxi to your hotel’s own dock solves it in one move.
03
The Lake Como road. From the town of Como to Tremezzo, Cadenabbia or Bellagio is narrow and takes 45 minutes to an hour. The boat is faster and far more beautiful.
04
Mountain permits. The road up the Alpe di Siusi is restricted to permit holders. The hotel and your driver handle it.
05
The Amalfi Coast road. Narrow, winding and busy all summer. Arrive by sea, or fly to Scala.
06
Timing between regions. Check-out, a train, a boat and check-in can turn into a lost day. I build every move door to door, with one person to message if anything changes.

Before you ask

Getting around Italy FAQ

Should we take the train or a private car between Rome, Florence and Venice?

Between Rome and Florence, and between Florence and Venice, the fast train usually wins: about an hour and a half and a little over two hours, against roughly three hours each by road. I only book it with a meet-and-greet and porters at both stations and a driver or water taxi waiting when you step off. Everywhere else, a private car, boat or helicopter is the better way.

Can you arrange helicopter transfers in Italy?

Yes. The Dolomites, Lake Como, Venice and the Amalfi Coast are where a helicopter earns its place, turning a long mountain or coast road into a short flight, weather permitting. The nearest helipad to Aman Venice is about eight minutes away, and on the Amalfi Coast it is in Scala, close to Ravello.

Do we need to rent a car in Italy?

No. In the cities a car is a liability, and in the countryside a private driver who knows the back roads is the better way to do wine-tasting days. If you want to drive for the pleasure of it, I’ll have a supercar waiting for a day in the hills.

How do we get to our hotel in Venice?

By private water taxi, straight from Marco Polo airport or Santa Lucia station to your hotel’s dock. No luggage over bridges and no vaporetto.

How do we get to Lake Como or the Amalfi Coast?

For the middle of Lake Como, a car from Milan to the water, then about 30 minutes by private boat to your hotel’s dock. For the Amalfi Coast, a private boat from Naples or Salerno for the hotels on the water, a private car for the rest, or a helicopter to Scala.
