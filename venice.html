Four of the city’s best hotels, toured in one day in October 2026. Who each one is for, which floor to ask for, and why Venice is the gateway to the Dolomites.

This is a very exciting time for Venice.

Airelles Palladio, Orient Express and the newly reopened Danieli under Four Seasons have joined Aman, the St. Regis, the Gritti Palace, Venice Venice and the Cipriani. Travelers still want to see Italy’s iconic cities, but they also want calm and serenity, and the best of these hotels deliver both.

I toured four of them in person in October 2026 and met with the teams on property. Here is where I would send my own clients, and why.

Hotel by hotel

Where I would stay in Venice

Three wowed me. One is a great option with a showstopping lobby. All four toured in person.

Giudecca · My number one in Italy

Airelles Palladio

Garden estate with pools (Airelles)

The most spectacular hotel I toured, and one of the most beautiful properties I have ever seen.

Set away from the crowds on Giudecca, five minutes by boat from everything. It is private and secure, yet lively, social and fun; sophisticated, never stuffy. The arrival is spectacular, the rooms are spacious and the gardens are truly serene.

The three-story spa is remarkable, the outdoor pool is stunning, and there are four restaurants and four bars. I worried it might feel isolated. It does not. The penthouse, the Suite del Doge, is 450 square meters with four bedrooms.

Perfect for Families, groups, couples who want a resort, anyone who values calm

Grand Canal · Toured in person

Aman Venice

Palazzo on the Grand Canal (Aman)

One of the most beautiful Amans I have seen, built around privacy and classic Aman service.

Twenty-four rooms in a 16th-century palazzo, with beautiful natural light, gardens on the canal and an understated restoration that lets the frescoes and silk walls speak. The general manager, Licinio Garavaglia, is genuinely a fantastic person, and I know he will take great care of my clients.

There is no pool and the spa is small, so this is a hotel for the city and for quiet. For families or groups, Coccina’s Apartment and the Grand Canal Apartment each have three bedrooms.

Perfect for Couples, privacy seekers, Aman loyalists, a Venice and Dolomites trip

Cannaregio · Toured in person

Orient Express Palazzo Donà Giovannelli

Restored Gothic palazzo (Orient Express)

“It is almost overwhelming how beautiful this property is.” I said that out loud on the tour.

An eight-year renovation of a 15th-century palace, and truly jaw dropping. Dark wood with some seventy kinds of marble, restored painted ceilings and chandeliers; everywhere I looked, something new caught my eye. I have not seen that much marble since the bathrooms at Villa San Michele.

The location is quiet but convenient, on a canal corner in Cannaregio, close to the train station and an easy walk into the city.

Perfect for Design lovers, couples, travelers arriving by train

San Marco · Toured in person

The Danieli, A Four Seasons Hotel

Historic grand hotel (Four Seasons)

The lobby is a showstopper, and the rooftop restaurant has one of the great views in Venice.

The Gothic atrium is the place to see and be seen, and Terrazza Danieli on the roof looks over the Salute, the Doge’s Palace and the lagoon. The rooms are understated and very classic.

The location cuts both ways: you are steps from St. Mark’s, and it is busy right outside the door. It is a bigger hotel, which makes service less personal, but also makes an upgrade more likely. A great option that did not quite wow me the way Aman and Airelles did.

Perfect for First-time visitors, Four Seasons loyalists, travelers who want to be at San Marco

Know before you go

The fine print, from your advisor

01
Ask for the piano nobile. In any palazzo, here or almost anywhere in Italy, I always want a room or suite on the noble floor: one level above the ground floor, the second floor in American terms. It is where the family lived, and the ceilings are much higher.
02
Historic means a little imperfect, and that is the point. Touring the penthouse at Airelles, I noticed a slight slope in one room, where the original structure could not be changed. Some might call that unfinished. I loved it. It shows how historic the building really is.
03
Three nights is the sweet spot. Enough for San Marco, the islands and a few long lunches. At Airelles Palladio I could easily do four.
04
Calm is the new luxury. Travelers still want the iconic cities, but they also want serenity. Airelles, Aman and Orient Express all deliver it flawlessly; the Danieli puts you in the middle of the action.
05
Arrive by water. I arrange a private water taxi from Marco Polo airport straight to your hotel’s dock, and private boats to Murano, Burano and Torcello.
Make it one trip

Venice, then the Dolomites.

Venice is the perfect gateway to the Dolomites, and I do not think a Dolomites trip is complete without two or three nights here first.

Aman makes it easy: two nights at Aman Venice and three at Aman Rosa Alpina, with transfers included. For the fastest connection I can arrange a helicopter; the nearest helipad is about eight minutes from Aman Venice.

Before you ask

Venice FAQ

What is the best luxury hotel in Venice?

For me, Airelles Palladio on Giudecca. It is the most beautiful hotel I have seen in Italy, with gardens, pools, a three-story spa, four restaurants and four bars, five minutes by boat from St. Mark’s. Aman Venice and Orient Express Palazzo Donà Giovannelli are close behind for travelers who want a smaller palazzo.

Which Venice hotel is best for families?

Airelles Palladio. It has outdoor and indoor pools, a kids club, room to roam, a three-bedroom suite with its own private pool and the four-bedroom Suite del Doge.

How many nights should I spend in Venice?

Three nights is the sweet spot. At Airelles Palladio I could easily do four. If Venice is the gateway to the Dolomites, two or three nights works well.

Which floor should I ask for in a Venetian palazzo?

The piano nobile, or noble floor: one level above the ground floor, the second floor in American terms. It was where the owning family lived, and the ceilings are much higher. That holds for palazzi across Italy.

Is Giudecca a good place to stay in Venice?

Yes, if you want calm. Giudecca faces Venice across the water, and the hotel boats reach St. Mark’s in about five minutes. You get peace and quiet in a very busy city.

Can I combine Venice with the Dolomites?

Yes, and I recommend it. Aman offers two nights at Aman Venice and three nights at Aman Rosa Alpina with transfers included, and I can arrange a helicopter between the two. Any Dolomites trip is better with two or three nights in Venice first.
