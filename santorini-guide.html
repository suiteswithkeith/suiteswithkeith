One Day On The Water, One Day Inland — Here's The Menu

Not to scale, but close — the dashed line is the Fira → Oia rim walk.

The Shape Of It

Santorini is a crescent around a flooded volcano. Everything with a view sits along the west rim — Fira, Imerovigli, Oia — and everything else worth your time, the vineyards, the ruins and the black-sand beaches, is on the gentle slope behind.

Getting Around

Private transfers

What I arrange by default for the airport and the ferry port — Athinios is a steep, chaotic switchback with a taxi queue you don't want.

A car for one day

Worth it for the inland day only. Parking in Oia and Fira is close to impossible in season.

Taxis are scarce

A few dozen on the whole island, and none you can hail. Pre-book the driver for a dinner at the other end of the island.

Oia to Ammoudi

Three hundred steps, shared with donkeys. Walk down if you like — book the car back up.

The One I'd Never Skip · Shared · 5 Hours

The caldera cruise

You cannot understand Santorini from the rim — you have to be under it. Every boat runs the same circuit: the caldera wall, the volcano and hot springs, the red and white beaches, back for sunset. What you're really choosing is boat size — fifty-five people, or fourteen with a proper cooked meal instead of a buffet.

From $87 per person · small boats from $121
Private · 5 Hours

A private catamaran

The whole boat and crew for your group — up to fourteen, just as good for two. You set the departure time, how long you stay in each cove, and whether you swim at the volcano or sail straight past it. The best value here for a family or a group of friends.

From €1,000 for the boat
Private · 4–5 Hours

A luxury day yacht

A different category altogether: a Pardo, a Wally or a Sunreef with a captain and a stewardess, chef's appetizers and good wine on board, chauffeured to the dock and back. For honeymoons and anniversaries — the day becomes the memory.

From $1,430 for the yacht
Inland · 1 Hour To Half A Day

Vassaltis, the winery

Assyrtiko grown the way it's grown nowhere else — vines coiled into low baskets in volcanic ash, no trellis, no irrigation. The wine is bone-dry, saline and unmistakable. A four-wine tasting, a shared lunch in the vines, or the full vineyard walk with a sommelier.

Tastings from $44 · vineyard lunch from $61
Morning · 10 KM · 3–4 Hours

Fira to Oia, on foot

The signature walk and the only way to understand the shape of the island — the whole crescent of the caldera on your left the entire way. Exposed and shadeless: start by eight, carry water, and have a car meet you in Oia rather than walking back.

The best free thing on the island
Full Day · Car Or Driver

The inland day

Akrotiri's buried Bronze Age town in the morning while it's cool, lunch and wine at the vineyard, Pyrgos for the late light — the highest village on the island, and almost nobody is up there. Dinner inland at Metaxi Mas, where the island actually eats.

The day people come home talking about

The boats and the best tables go weeks ahead in summer — from mid-June on, three to four weeks out is not too early. Send me the ones you want and I'll build the days around them, and hold whatever fills up first.

Where I'd Eat

Six Tables, In The Order I'd Book Them

Omnia at Canaves Epitome best meal on the island

The best meal I had in Greece, and where I'd send you for sunset — Epitome faces an actual sunset into the Aegean rather than a caldera view. It's also one of the harder tables to get at the hour worth having.

Lycabettus at Andronis the big-occasion table

The famous cliffside room. If you're staying at Andronis, book it the moment your suite is confirmed. If you're not, it's still worth giving an evening to.

Varoulko at Grace, Imerovigli most elegant dinner

Michelin-starred chef Lefteris Lazarou's seafood tasting menu, and he personally visits tables. Distinctly Greek yet modern — the strongest part of the Grace experience.

Selene, Fira best modern Greek

The island's benchmark for a serious tasting menu — Santorini fava, white eggplant, tomatoes, Assyrtiko. Worth the night away from Oia, and worth a driver to get you back.

Metaxi Mas, Exo Gonia where the island eats

Inland, away from the caldera entirely, and full of Greeks rather than honeymooners. Long lunches, house wine, and the kind of cooking the rim charges four times as much for. Book it — everyone has worked this one out by now.

Armeni, below Oia best lunch on the water

A handful of tables at the water's edge in the little port below Oia, grilled fish straight off the boat. Go at lunch rather than sunset — same food, a fraction of the crowd, and you won't be climbing back up 300 steps in the dark.

Book these four to eight weeks ahead in high season — from mid-June through mid-September the tables worth having are gone weeks out, and what's left is 6:00pm or 10:30pm. Send me the ones you want and I'll go through your hotel's concierge.

Santorini · The Sunset

Where I'd Actually Watch It

Everyone is told to go to the castle at Oia, so everyone does — several thousand people standing shoulder to shoulder on a wall, phones up, for a sunset you can see better from almost anywhere else on the island. Go once if you must. Then do one of these instead.

Your own terrace

The reason you paid for the room. Order a bottle, stay put, and watch the whole thing without moving — no crowd, no walk home in the dark, and the best seat on the island is the one you already booked.

Skaros, Imerovigli

The short walk out to the old rock takes about twenty minutes and leaves the crowds behind almost immediately. You get the caldera from an angle most visitors never see, and Oia in the distance rather than around you.

From a boat

Golden hour with a glass of Assyrtiko and roughly two hundred fewer people around you. The cleanest way to avoid the scrum entirely, and the one excursion I recommend to essentially everyone.

Pyrgos, inland

The highest village on the island, and it faces the whole thing — sunset on one side, the caldera on the other, and a village that's still a village. Have dinner up here and let the rim empty out below you.

Things I'd Tell A Friend

Five Things, Before You Book Anything

  1. Book dinner before you land. The best tables go four to eight weeks out, and this is the single thing most people leave too late.
  2. Get off the rim once. Pyrgos, the vineyards, the black-sand south — the island is more than the caldera, and a day away from it is the day people remember.
  3. Two things a day, maximum. Santorini is small but slow. Chasing a list is how a good trip turns into a tiring one.
  4. See the caldera from the water. The one excursion I recommend to everyone — here's how I'd do it.
  5. Never fly home the day you sail. Build in the buffer night. The Aegean does not care about your itinerary — the transfer cheat sheet covers the rest.
