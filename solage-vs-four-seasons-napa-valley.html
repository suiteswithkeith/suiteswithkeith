Head-to-head · The Calistoga question

Solage or Four Seasons?

Both sit in Calistoga, at the quiet, hot-springs far north of the valley — one is the design icon that defined California wine country luxury, the other a working-vineyard resort run at full Four Seasons standard. #04 and #02 on my ranking; here’s the split.

#04 and #02 in my Napa & Sonoma ranking · both stayed in person · both Calistoga

Choose Solage

If relaxed is the point

Iconic Calistoga-modern design — the template half the newer properties are chasing — with a private garden or terrace on most rooms, some with private hot tubs, and rates that meaningfully undercut the top three.

Choose Four Seasons

If you want the full five-star apparatus

Set within its own working vineyard, with service at genuine Four Seasons standard on every stay I’ve had, a one-star Michelin restaurant, and a pool bar I love. The rare wine country hotel that fits almost everyone.

Fine print on both: Solage’s spa — a signature here — is due for a refresh, worth knowing as you plan treatments. At Four Seasons, request an upper floor if outdoor-shower privacy matters; some ground-floor showers face the pathways. And Calistoga means a longer drive to the southern wineries — plan tastings accordingly.

The question behind the question

Same town, two different trips.

Solage is a fantastic hotel — just not quite at the level of the three above it, and it isn’t trying to be. If you want real terrace privacy, that iconic design, and the strongest cost-to-experience math in the valley, it’s the pick — especially for younger groups who’d find white-glove formality a drag.

Four Seasons is what you book when consistency is the luxury: service that separates it from everything below it, a Michelin star on property, and a resort that genuinely works for families, couples, and groups alike.
