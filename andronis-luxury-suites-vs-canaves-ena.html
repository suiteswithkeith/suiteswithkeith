Head-to-head · Oia, Santorini

Andronis Luxury Suites or Canaves Ena?

The refined flagship of one Oia dynasty against the freshly renovated original of the other — #04 and #05 on my Santorini ranking, and closer than those numbers suggest. I toured both on the same trip.

#04 and #05 on my Santorini ranking · both toured · both preferred partners

Choose Luxury Suites

If polish and ease win

Spacious suites with a water feature in all 39 rooms, the Mare Sanus Spa, Lycabettus at home, and the portfolio’s most manageable stairs — a few suites sit only 15–20 steps from check-in.

Choose Ena

If character and intimacy win

Eighteen suites carved from 17th-century wine caves, run by the same family since the 80s and brought fully up to date by a 2024 renovation. Higher on the Caldera — which buys real privacy — with a swim-up bar that adds rare social energy.

Ena is framed as the accessible Canaves, but the value positioning doesn’t always show up in the rate — pricing almost always lands right where Andronis Boutique does. Judge it on the room, the privacy, the water feature, and the price for your actual dates.

The question behind the question

Flagship polish or family heritage — this one is a mood choice.

If a water feature is non-negotiable, or stairs are a concern, Luxury Suites settles it — every one of its 39 rooms has a plunge pool, jacuzzi, or full pool, and it’s my pick in the portfolio when mobility is a factor.

But Ena has the most personal story on the Caldera — one family since the 80s, suites that trace to 17th-century wine caves — and the team there, especially longtime sales director Des, is a big reason I trust sending clients to the property. The intimacy is the point; just know the square footage isn’t the selling feature.
