Athens

Everything I’d actually book here — guides, food, museums and tables — whether you have two nights or a week.

Start Here

Two nights, or two weeks

Most people give Athens a night on the way to the islands and regret it. Give it two and it becomes part of the trip; give it three and it becomes the part people talk about afterwards.

One

Do the ancient city with a guide

This is the one place in Greece where a private guide changes everything — the ruins are rubble without someone to tell you what you’re looking at, and you skip every line.

Two

Then stop sightseeing

One morning of antiquity a day is plenty. The rest of Athens is neighborhoods, long lunches and rooftops, and that’s the half people are surprised by.

Three

Start early, rest at midday

From June to September the middle of the day on the rock is genuinely punishing. Everything below is written around an eight o’clock start and a slow afternoon.

The Shape Of It

The city, and where everything sits

Central Athens is smaller than people expect — the Acropolis, the Agora, Plaka, Monastiraki and the museum are all within a twenty-minute walk of each other. Kolonaki and Pangrati are the two worth a taxi. Tap any pin for what it is.

Bookable · One

The ancient city

Three versions of the same morning, and the only real question is how much time you want to give it. All are private, all are led by a state-licensed guide, and all start early enough to be off the rock before the heat and the cruise groups arrive. Hotel pickup is available on each.

Acropolis & Acropolis Museum

The complete version and the one I book most: the rock first thing — Propylaea, Parthenon, Erechtheion, the Nike temple — then down to the museum to see the sculpture that came off it, with the same guide joining the two halves together.

Departs daily at 7:45am. Four hours, pickup available, and you are finished before the worst of the day.

Half day · On your own

The Ancient Agora

Quieter than the Acropolis and, to me, more moving — the market and law courts where the city actually ran, with the best-preserved temple in Greece standing over it. Easy to do unguided after a morning above.

An hour

Temple of Zeus & the Stadium

Fifteen colossal columns of what was the largest temple in Greece, Hadrian’s Arch beside them, and the all-marble Panathenaic Stadium ten minutes further on. A good late-afternoon walk on the way to Pangrati.

Practical

A note on tickets and heat

The Acropolis now runs on timed entry with a daily cap, and midsummer afternoons are sometimes closed outright. Every guided option above includes the timed ticket — another reason not to do this one yourselves.

On Foot

The neighborhoods

Athens rewards wandering more than almost any European capital, because the good parts aren’t signposted. Four neighborhoods, each an afternoon, and none of them more than a fifteen-minute walk or a five-minute taxi from the last.

Morning

Plaka and Anafiotika

The old quarter under the rock, and above it the pocket of whitewashed island houses built by masons from Anafi — a corner of the Cyclades hidden in the middle of Athens. Go before ten and it’s yours.

Afternoon & evening

Monastiraki and Psyrri

Flea market and souvlaki by day, and after dark the old workshop streets of Psyrri filling up with bars and tables. Loud, young and the most fun part of the city at night.

Afternoon

Kolonaki

The smart end of Athens — boutiques, galleries and cafes on the slope below Lycabettus, and the natural place to spend the hours before a serious dinner.

Evening

Pangrati

Leafy, residential and where the interesting young kitchens have moved. Nothing to see, which is exactly the point — come for dinner and stay in the square afterwards.

Morning · Book ahead

The central market, with a guide

Varvakios and the spice shops of Evripidou Street — the loudest, most alive part of Athens, and much better with someone who can order for you. A food-focused morning here is the best value guiding in the city.

Where I’d stay

Koukaki

Residential and unglamorous in the best way, five minutes below the Acropolis Museum. Neighborhood tavernas, good coffee, and you can walk home from everything.

Indoors

The museums worth your time

Athens has dozens. These are the three I’d actually send you to — and all three are gloriously air-conditioned in August.

Two hours

National Archaeological Museum

The greatest collection of Greek antiquities anywhere — the Mycenaean gold, the bronze Poseidon, the Antikythera mechanism. It’s north of the center and worth the taxi.

90 minutes

Acropolis Museum

Glass floors over a live excavation, and the Parthenon frieze laid out at full scale with the rock itself framed through the windows. Best seen the same day as the Acropolis, and after it.

An hour

Benaki Museum

Greek art and craft from antiquity to the twentieth century in a fine old Kolonaki house. Smaller, human-scaled, and a good hour before dinner up that end of town.

Bookable · Two

Out of the city

Three ways out of the center, asking for very different amounts of your day: an afternoon on the coast, a half-day for sunset, or a full day north into the mountains. With two nights in Athens I’d only do the beach. With three, add one of the others.

Full day · The Riviera · Book ahead

A beach day at Manko

Most people don’t realize Athens has a coastline this good twenty-five minutes from the Acropolis. Manko sits on the sand on the Riviera below Vouliagmeni — loungers and umbrellas down to the water, a long Mediterranean lunch, and the sea going gold in the late afternoon. Arrive late morning, stay until the light changes.

The right thing to do on an arrival day, a departure day, or any day the heat makes sightseeing miserable. Sunbeds and tables both need reserving in season.

Half day · Private car · Book ahead

Cape Sounion & the Temple of Poseidon

An hour and a half down the coast road to a white marble temple on a headland with the sea on three sides. Leave mid-afternoon, arrive for the light, watch the sun go into the Aegean from between the columns, and have dinner at a fish taverna on the way back.

The most romantic half-day in Attica, and the easiest to arrange — a private car and driver is all it takes.

Full day · Private guide · Book ahead

Delphi

Two and a half hours north, on the slopes of Mount Parnassus — the sanctuary of Apollo, the theatre and the stadium stacked up a mountainside above a valley of olive trees running to the sea. The ancient world considered this the center of the earth, and standing there you understand why.

A long day — ten to eleven hours door to door. Worth every one of them, with a guide and a car, not a coach.

Athens

Rooftops and sunset

The Acropolis is lit every night and visible from half the roofs in the city. This is the hour Athens is at its best — plan one of these into every evening.

Filopappou Hill

Pine paths and smooth marble outcrops directly opposite the rock. Free, uncrowded, and the sunset the locals actually use. Bring something to drink and go up an hour before.

Lycabettus Hill

The highest point in Athens, reached by funicular from Kolonaki. The whole city below you, the Acropolis in the middle of it, and the sea beyond Piraeus.

A Monastiraki roof

Every building on the square has a bar on top and the same extraordinary view. A drink before dinner rather than dinner itself — the cooking rarely matches the outlook.

Areopagus Rock

The marble outcrop just below the Acropolis entrance, where Athens has watched the sun go down for three thousand years. Busy, spectacular, and genuinely slippery — flat shoes.

The Tables

Where I’d eat

Athens is the best eating in Greece — better than any island, and a fraction of the price. Tell me which of these appeal and we’ll work through your hotel’s concierge to get them booked.

Pangrati · The big night

Spondi

The most serious kitchen in the city, in a vaulted courtyard away from the sights. The one to build an evening around.

Rooftop · Special occasion

Hytra

Ideal for a special night, with incredible Acropolis views — modern Greek cooking that actually deserves the setting rather than coasting on it.

Syntagma · The classic view

GB Roof Garden

On top of the Grande Bretagne, looking straight across at the lit Acropolis. The most reliable grand-hotel evening in Athens — go for a drink at minimum.

The one everyone wants

Pharaoh

Probably the coolest restaurant in Athens right now — amazing food, fun atmosphere, and hard to get into. Worth asking your concierge the day you arrive.

Central · Lunch or dinner

Nolan

Creative Greek-Japanese sharing plates in a relaxed room. The easiest good meal in the city, and my usual first-night suggestion.

Psyrri · Romantic

Linou Soumpasis

A beautiful hidden courtyard, and perfect for a romantic dinner. The kind of place you’d never find on your own.

Metaxourgeio · Garden

Aleria

A beautiful garden setting for a quieter, romantic dinner — the alternative to a rooftop when you want to actually hear each other.

Thissio · Modern Greek

Kuzina

Inventive Greek cooking on the pedestrian street below the Acropolis, with a roof terrace looking up at it. A long-standing favorite of mine.

Hilton area · Five chefs

Cookoovaya

Five well-known chefs, one open kitchen, and consistently excellent modern Greek. Big, buzzy and easy to like.

Tasting menu

CTC Urban Gastronomy

A small, ambitious tasting-menu room for a food-led evening. Book it if you like being surprised course by course.

Below the Acropolis · The view

Dionysos Zonar’s

Directly opposite the rock, and the classic Athens lunch view. Old-school, comfortable, and exactly right on a first day.

Late · Lively

Birdman

A fun local spot for yakitori, cocktails and a lively vibe. Where to go when dinner ran short and the night hasn’t.

Athens books later than you’d think — nine o’clock is normal and ten is not late. For Spondi, Hytra, Pharaoh and CTC, ask your concierge two to three weeks out; those four fill up first.

How I’d spend them

One morning of antiquity, one morning of ordinary life, and both afternoons free. Add a third day and it goes to Delphi or Sounion.

Day One · The rock

The private Acropolis and museum tour at 7:45, finished by midday. Long lunch below the rock, an hour out of the sun, then Plaka and Anafiotika in the late afternoon and up Filopappou for sunset.

Dinner: Nolan, or Kuzina for the terrace

Day Two · The living city

The central market in the morning, ideally with a guide, then the Ancient Agora on the way back. Afternoon in Kolonaki or the Benaki, drinks on a Monastiraki roof as the Acropolis lights come on.

Dinner: Spondi, Hytra or Pharaoh — the big one

A third day · Out of town

Delphi if you want the day to be the event, Cape Sounion if you’d rather sleep in and drive down the coast for the sunset. Both need a car and a driver, and I’ll arrange either.
