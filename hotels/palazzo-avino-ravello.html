The review

A historic pink palazzo in quiet Ravello, with sweeping views and a beautiful pool, for travelers who would trade the beach for a real village.

I visited Palazzo Avino in October 2026. It is a beautiful, historic and very pink property up in Ravello, which is relatively quiet compared to Amalfi and Positano. The rooms have sweeping mountain and sea views, and the tiered gardens step down to a beautiful pool overlooking the town below.

It is also significantly less expensive than most of the Amalfi Coast hotels I book. Of the ones I recommend, only Miramalfi comes in lower, and I would actually rank Miramalfi above it.

The big thing to know is the location. The beach club is 15 to 20 minutes down a very windy, steep road, so you are not waking up and walking to the beach. There is also a fairly unsightly building below the property, which may bother some people.

I will be honest about the mood, too. The vibe felt a bit awkward to me, and I never fully settled in. Part of that may be that it was very quiet while I was there, especially in the indoor common areas. The food I had was not a highlight either.

Where Palazzo Avino shines is Ravello itself: an authentic Italian village that is not overrun by day-trippers. For travelers who want seclusion, and maybe do not have the budget to be right on the water, it works well. It also pairs nicely with a coastal hotel, three nights here and three nights by the sea.

Know before you book

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

01
The beach is a drive away. The beach club is 15 to 20 minutes down a windy, steep road. If walking down to the water matters, look at Hotel Santa Caterina, Il San Pietro or Miramalfi.
02
Mind the view below. A fairly unsightly building sits below the property. Some guests will not mind it, but I would rather you hear it from me.
03
It is very quiet. Especially in the indoor common areas. If you want energy and a scene, Positano is the other end of the spectrum.
04
Split the stay. Three nights in Ravello and three on the water is the version I would book: the village and the views here, then a hotel where you can swim off the rocks.
05
Weighing it against the Caruso? Belmond Hotel Caruso is also in Ravello. I have not been inside yet (it was closed for a private event when I tried), but it runs around $1,000 more per night, its base rooms are about double the size of Palazzo Avino’s, and I expect the service is slightly more elevated. It is really a different product.
