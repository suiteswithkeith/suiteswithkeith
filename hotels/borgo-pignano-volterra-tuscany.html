The review

A restored hilltop borgo outside Volterra — stone, lavender and a valley view that turns gold every evening.

Borgo Pignano is a villa and a small stone village set on a hilltop outside Volterra, restored and run as one quiet estate. There is no town to walk into and no crowd to work around — just the borgo, its gardens and the hills rolling away in every direction.

The view is the reason to come. Dinner is served under a reed-roofed pergola that frames the valley like a painting, and the pool sits on a terrace just below the villa, looking out over the same landscape.

I’ve slept here, not just toured it, so I can tell you which rooms and views are worth asking for. It’s my pick for couples and for anyone who wants a slower, more private Tuscany than the Chianti circuit.

What stood out

The details that made the list

01
Dinner under the pergola. Tables set under a reed roof with the valley laid out in front of you — book it for sunset, when the light goes gold.
02
The pool terrace. Tucked below the villa and cut into the hillside, with loungers facing the view.
03
A real borgo. Stone houses, a courtyard and a stairway up to the villa — it feels like a village, not a resort.
04
Quiet. Far enough from the main routes that the loudest thing at night is the wind in the lavender.
Know before you book

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

01
You’ll want a car (or a driver). It’s about an hour and a half from Florence. Volterra, San Gimignano and my other Tuscany estates are all within easy reach; I arrange drivers who know the back roads.
02
Stay longer than one night. This is a place to settle into. Plan for at least two or three nights so the pool, the dinners and the drives out don’t compete.
03
There’s a sister house in Florence. Borgo Pignano also runs a Florence address — I’ve stayed at both and can pair the city with the countryside in one trip.
04
Comparing Tuscany estates? Castiglion del Bosco is the big wine-and-golf estate; Castello di Casole is the traditional castle. Borgo Pignano is the quieter, more intimate choice.
