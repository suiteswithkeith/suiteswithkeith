The St. Regis Rome

Belle Époque grandeur and a nightly champagne sabrage ritual — for guests who want a bit more spectacle.

BelleÉpoque Grandeur — My Spectacle Pick In Rome
NightlyChampagne Sabrage Ritual
Inquire To Book

The third seat in my featured Rome trio belongs to the St. Regis, and its assignment is the opposite of understatement: Belle Époque grandeur and a nightly champagne sabrage ritual, for guests who want a bit more spectacle.

This is Rome's original grand hotel — opened by César Ritz himself in 1894 — restored to full lustre with chandeliers, frescoes, and a lobby that treats every arrival like an event. The nightly sabrage, a saber through a champagne bottle at aperitivo hour, sets the tone: theatrical on purpose.

Some trips call for the hidden garden; some call for the show. When a client is celebrating — a big birthday, a proposal, a vow renewal — this is the Rome hotel that plays the part with total commitment. St. Regis butler service comes standard, and Marriott loyalists get real value from their status here.

What Stood Out

The Details That Made The List

The grandeur. César Ritz's 1894 original, restored — chandeliers, frescoes, and the most theatrical interiors in Rome.

The nightly sabrage. A champagne bottle opened by saber every evening — the ritual that tells you what kind of hotel you're in.

Butler service standard. The St. Regis signature, from unpacking to pressing — spectacle backed by substance.

Know Before You Book

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

The neighborhood is grand, not charming. Near Repubblica and the Baths of Diocletian — monumental Rome rather than winding-lane Rome. A short taxi covers the difference.

If spectacle isn't the brief, my other picks win. The de Russie for understated elegance, the Eden for quiet romance — my featured page compares the trio.
