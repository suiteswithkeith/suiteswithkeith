The review

The real Italian Riviera — a restored grand hotel in a town where the Milanese still summer, at about half the price of Portofino.

Grand Hotel Miramare sits on the seafront of Santa Margherita Ligure, one of the most authentic coastal towns left in Italy. This is where Milanese families have spent their summers for generations. In the restaurants, the bars and the little tavernas, the language you hear is Italian. You’re not surrounded by other Americans, and that is exactly what most people are hoping for when they come to Italy.

The town is a real one. Fishmongers sell the morning catch to people who live there, the restaurants are fantastic and the bars stay lively late into the evening. That’s the difference from Amalfi, and from Portofino right next door, where the harbor is lined with Loro Piana and Dior and not much feels local anymore.

That’s why I now point a lot of clients here instead of the Belmond Splendido. The Miramare is the better version of the same idea: a better location, a far more authentic town, and about half the price, while still a very elevated hotel. There’s a big pool, a gym, a spa, a fantastic restaurant out on the terrace and a great bar with great service. Housekeeping was outstanding. Portofino is still a short ride away for lunch, but you’ll be glad to come home to Santa Margherita at night.

The hotel dates back to the early 1900s, and the renovation is beautiful. Most of the rooms are already restored, and the rest should be done by next summer. They kept the Italian character, so it feels like what it is: a historic grand hotel, not a new build. I’ve stayed here, so I can tell you which rooms to ask for.

What stood out

The details that made the list

01
You’re vacationing with Italians. Santa Margherita is where Milan goes to the sea. Restaurants, bars and the beach are full of Italian families, not tour groups — the authentic coastal Italy people are looking for and rarely find.
02
A renovation that kept the soul. The restored rooms are beautiful, and they still feel Italian and historic rather than generically modern. The remaining rooms are set to be finished by next summer.
03
The beach club. One of the largest beach clubs in Liguria, being restored for next summer. You can swim right off the beach or off the platforms. It’s open to the public, but in practice that means local Milanese families who have kept the same cabanas for generations. I think that’s a plus: you’re at the beach with real Italians.
04
A building that still follows its original plan. In the early 20th century, the noble families lived on the lower floors and the servants lived on the upper ones. The structure can’t be changed, so it still works that way: the lower floors have the larger rooms with real outdoor terraces, and rooms get smaller as you go up.
05
The top-floor suite. I stayed in a suite on the top floor with a wraparound balcony and stunning views of the sea. It’s one of the most beautiful, relaxing hotel rooms I’ve ever stayed in.
06
Even the entry-level rooms are spacious. Rooms in the base category are genuinely big. Above them sit around six beautiful signature suites.
07
Garden-view rooms that are actually good. These aren’t rooms looking at another building. They face a real garden, so they’re private, quiet and relaxing, with massive, tall ceilings. I’d still recommend a sea view, but if you don’t need one, the garden rooms are a great alternative.
08
Everything you need on property. A large pool, a gym, a spa, a fantastic terrace restaurant and a bar with great service. Housekeeping was outstanding all stay.

Know before you book

The Fine Print, From Your Advisor

01
The floor you’re on matters more than the room name. Ask me before you book. The hotel’s ground floor sits a full level above the street, and its “first floor” is higher again. So even the lowest rooms have a true sea view, and the floor numbers don’t mean what an American would expect. If you want space and a large outdoor terrace, the lower floors are the smart choice, because that’s where the noble families once lived. The upper floors are smaller, and the newest top floor, added after a fire, wasn’t allowed balconies at all. The exception is a couple of signature suites, including the one with a wraparound balcony I stayed in. Reach out and I’ll walk you through it and put you in the right room.
02
Book the sea view if you can. It’s the one I’d always recommend. If the sea view doesn’t fit the budget or the dates, the garden-view rooms are quiet, private and still a lovely stay.
03
It works very well for families. Certain suites can be combined behind one closed door, so you get two proper suites as one private unit. Some rooms with a sofa bed take up to three adults, or two adults and two children under 10.
04
The beach club is open to the public. Don’t let that put you off. In practice it means Italian families who have had their cabanas for generations. It’s part of what makes a stay here feel real.
05
Portofino is a day trip, not a base. Go for lunch, a walk around the harbor or a boat along the coast to San Fruttuoso and Camogli, then come back to Santa Margherita for dinner. I arrange boats and drivers.
Build the trip

The Italy honeymoon I’d plan for myself

The Miramare is the coastal leg that ties northern Italy together. It’s more affordable than Amalfi and it sits right between Lake Como and Tuscany. These are the routes I’d build around it. I handle every piece: the hotels, the rooms and every private transfer in between, timed to your check-outs and check-ins. You never touch a train timetable or a rental car.

For honeymooners

Lake Como, the Riviera, Tuscany and Florence

Arrive
Fly into Milan. A private driver meets you and takes you straight to the lake.
3 nights
Lake Como. The romantic start: Passalacqua, Villa d’Este or Il Sereno.
3–4 nights
Santa Margherita Ligure. Grand Hotel Miramare, the beach club, and Portofino by boat for lunch.
4–5 nights
Tuscany. A countryside borgo: Il Borro is my honeymoon pick, or see all three borghi side by side.
3 nights
Florence. End in the city at Four Seasons Firenze or Villa San Michele, then fly home.

Two weeks, four completely different Italys, and no logistics on your plate.

For families

The Riviera, Tuscany and Florence

Arrive
Fly into Milan. Straight down to the coast by private car.
4–5 nights
Santa Margherita Ligure. Combined suites at the Miramare, the pool and the beach club.
4–5 nights
Tuscany. A villa with space to spread out, like Borgo Pignano.
3 nights
Florence. Finish in the city, then fly home.

I’d skip Lake Como with kids. It’s at its best as a romantic stop, and the coast and the countryside give children far more to do.
