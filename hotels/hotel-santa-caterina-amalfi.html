The review

A guest in someone’s home.

Hotel Santa Caterina began as a six-room hotel, started by the town doctor of Amalfi. It opened in 1904 and has stayed in the family ever since. Today it is owned by his granddaughters, Giusi and Carmela “Ninni” Gambardella, the third generation, and Giusi and Carmela’s children, the fourth generation, are part of it now too.

What struck me most is the people. So many of the team have been here 15 to 20 years or more, and Giusi and Carmela are like their grandmothers or second mothers. They are still very much involved. When I brought up a client of mine who had stayed, Signora Giusi remembered them right away, down to the room numbers.

The luxury here is quiet and understated, and it lives in the tiny details. You dial 260 to reach the front desk. There is no WhatsApp, and reception still keeps the calendar and notes in real books with real pens. In a world of constant tech, that face-to-face connection really means something.

I stayed in room 45, in a fantastic little building just to the left of the main property. So private and serene, truly a dream. I spent a full day here the way my clients would: coffee in bed, the gym by the sea, a swim, room service, another swim, wandering the hotel. I never felt bored. I have stayed at more than 45 hotels in Italy this past month, all of them beautiful, and Santa Caterina is one of the few that left me with a feeling I can’t quite describe. It is better to leave when you’re sad to leave. I will be back, for five nights at least.

Casa Marchesa

The private annex.

About 300 meters from the main hotel is Casa Marchesa, Santa Caterina’s more modern sister house. It has 12 rooms in total, including a three-bedroom villa. The finishes are modern, but it still feels very Santa Caterina.

Every room has a sea view, they are incredibly private, and Casa Marchesa has its own pool just for its guests. If you want the family warmth of Santa Caterina with a little more privacy and a newer look, this is where I would put you. The three-bedroom villa is a natural fit for families and friends traveling together.

Before you ask

The questions I get

Is Hotel Santa Caterina still family-owned?

Yes. It began as a six-room hotel, started by the town doctor of Amalfi, opened in 1904, and has stayed in the Gambardella family ever since. Today it is run by his granddaughters, sisters Giusi and Carmela “Ninni,” the third generation, with Giusi and Carmela’s children, the fourth generation, now part of it too.

What makes Santa Caterina different from other Amalfi Coast hotels?

It feels like being a guest in someone's home. Many of the team have been there 15 to 20 years or more, the owner is still very involved, and the luxury is quiet and in the tiny details rather than in the spectacle.

Is Santa Caterina old-fashioned?

In the best way. You dial 260 for the front desk instead of sending a WhatsApp, and reception still keeps the calendar in real books with real pens. If you want face-to-face service, this is the place.

Santa Caterina or Borgo Santandrea?

Borgo Santandrea is spectacular and brand new in feel. Santa Caterina is the opposite: it has been here for over a century and it knows exactly what it is doing. If you want warmth and history over a statement, choose Santa Caterina.

Is Santa Caterina good for families and multigenerational trips?

Yes. Families come back for generations. I met a guest on a multigenerational trip whose grandparents had been coming since the 1960s.

Which room should I book at Santa Caterina?

The categories I book are the Special Suites, the Full Sea View Garden Suites, the front sea view Junior Suites and the Front Sea View Doubles on the upper floors. I stayed in room 45, in a lovely little building just to the left of the main hotel. It was so private and serene, truly a dream. Tell me what matters most to you and I will request the right room.

What is Casa Marchesa?

Santa Caterina's more modern annex, about 300 meters from the main hotel. It has 12 rooms in total, including a three-bedroom villa, all with sea views, very private, and with a pool just for Casa Marchesa guests. It still feels very Santa Caterina.

How many nights should I stay at Santa Caterina?

At least five. It is the kind of hotel you want to slow down in, and you will still be sad to leave.
