The review

A Grade I listed Piccadilly mansion reborn as a hotel that feels like a private residence, opening February 2027.

I toured Cambridge House in a hard hat in November 2026, a few months before it opens. It’s a Grade I listed mansion on Piccadilly, begun in the 1750s for the Earl of Egremont, who died before it was finished; his widow became its first resident. It was later the Naval and Military Club for well over a century, until 1999, and has sat empty ever since.

Now it’s been restored by the Reuben family and will be run by Auberge Collection. The idea is a hotel that doesn’t feel like a hotel: you arrive through a private forecourt, and the spaces are called the hall, the study and the gallery, as if it were a private house.

In feel, it sits between the history of The Lanesborough and the modernity of The Chancery Rosewood: the architecture is all heritage, and the furnishings are contemporary. The interiors are by Jean-Louis Deniot (classic, a little feminine) and Laura Gonzalez (moody and textural), and the Reuben family’s private art collection, with works by Richard Prince, Sterling Ruby and Robert Rauschenberg, will be shown publicly for the first time.

What stood out

The details that made the list

01
The Piano Nobile. Six signature suites, each individually decorated and named for a figure from the house’s history (the Augusta Suite has Wedgwood and pearl beading). They can all connect into a private floor of about 6,500 square feet with six bedrooms.
02
The largest suite. The Dolphus Suite, about 1,200 square feet with a private terrace over the courtyard.
03
Five places to eat and drink. The In and Out Bar for coffee and cocktails between inside and out; the Rose Room and Bay Room for all-day dining and a contemporary afternoon tea; Majors, the signature restaurant with Major Food Group (inspired by The Grill in New York), with the Orangerie for private dining; and the Reuben Bar, with Asian-inspired food and a cigar lounge.
04
A Roman bathhouse underground. A wellbeing space across three subterranean floors, built around water rituals, with poolside programming and arts and culture events.
05
Shepherd Market next door. One of the last real villages in Mayfair, now cafés, restaurants, members’ clubs and galleries.
