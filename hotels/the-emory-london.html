The review

Sixty suites on Hyde Park, everything included, and four floors of wellness.

All 60 rooms at The Emory are suites, starting at 50 square meters, which is huge for London. The building is by Richard Rogers (the Pompidou Centre, the Lloyd’s Building), suspended on its structure so you never feel the Piccadilly Line running underneath, and it won a 2026 RIBA award.

Six designers each did two floors, all on a warm wood-grain theme, so the rooms vary in tone and style. Find a floor you love and you can ask for it again next time.

Then there’s what’s included: the full minibar, alcohol too, and a bottle of wine or champagne each day, chosen from a list through your butler. Add four floors of wellness, with resident doctors, and it’s a different kind of London hotel. It suits privacy-seeking clients best, celebrities and anyone avoiding crowds.

What stood out

The details that made the list

01
Everything is included. The full minibar, alcohol included, plus a daily bottle of wine or champagne.
02
A butler on WhatsApp. Multilingual, reachable by message, and your preferences and dietary needs are remembered for next time. In-room dining was curated in part by Rosie Huntington-Whiteley.
03
Four floors of wellness. A Tracy Anderson studio, a members’ club with health-focused food and drink, and two resident doctors three days a week (one was a GP to Queen Elizabeth II for 30 years) offering full health checks and longevity advice.
04
The Corner Park Suite. The standout room, big enough for a proper dining table.
05
New Year’s Eve. Free-flowing champagne for in-house guests, with views of the London Eye fireworks.
