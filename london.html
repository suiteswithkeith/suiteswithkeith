Plan my London stay

The first London decision isn’t which hotel. It’s whether this is your first trip or your fifth.

First-timers want classic London in a central spot, with the big sights easy to reach. Return visitors usually want something smaller and quieter, in a neighborhood where they can live like a Londoner for a few days.

I stayed at 45 Park Lane and visited the rest in person in November 2026. Here is where I would send my own clients, and why. I’ll add the rest of my London visits here after the trip.

Park Lane, Mayfair · Stayed November 2026

45 Park Lane

Moody, intimate townhouse (Dorchester Collection)

Moody, sexy and small, with Dorchester-quality beds and service. The suites are all velvet sofas, soft textured walls and brass, and the marble bathrooms are stunning. It’s the kind of hotel where the small things are already handled before you think to ask.

Right for
Couples, design lovers, repeat guests who want to be remembered, travelers who want Dorchester service at a smaller scale
Room I’d book
A suite, for the bathroom alone.Stunning marble, heated floors and Toto toilets. I stayed in a seventh-floor suite.
Booking with me
8am check-in and 8pm check-out, guaranteed.Only when you book through me.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

8am check-in, 8pm check-out, guaranteed. When you book through me, your room is ready at 8am and yours until 8pm. Land on the overnight flight and go straight to bed, or take the evening flight home without losing the day.

Personal attention, remembered. A hotel this size gives incredible personal attention, almost like Portrait in Florence and Milan. It’s a hotel for someone who likes to come back and know it will remember every little thing about them.

A real spa and gym. The spa is a major selling point, with a pool beside a floral mosaic wall, and the gym is super expansive, one of the nicer city-hotel gyms I’ve seen.

The little things. Oat milk and regular milk already in the fridge. A small touch, but it means coffee first thing in the morning without calling down.

A host on every floor. Around the clock, with butler service from the one-bedroom suites up, plus private entrances including one for the ninth-floor penthouse.

Park view, guaranteed. Every room faces Hyde Park, guaranteed through me. From my seventh-floor suite, though, the view wasn’t the main event.

The verdict

Dorchester-quality beds and service in a moody, intimate hotel.

Park Lane, Mayfair · Visited November 2026

The Dorchester

Renovated Park Lane grande dame (Dorchester Collection)

Completely renovated since 2022, from the lighting to the moldings. Where it used to be brown, navy and heavy wood, it’s now quintessentially British without being stuffy: bright, with a real sense of place, and a different color palette on each floor.

Right for
Food lovers, celebrations, travelers who want classic British style that feels bright and current

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Sushi Kanazawa. A hidden 13-seat omakase that earned a Michelin star within six months, with everything brought in from Japan.

Three Michelin stars. Alain Ducasse at The Dorchester, plus The Grill and afternoon tea in the Promenade.

The Vesper Bar. Live music, a low-table dining menu and a semi-private snug.

Shared spa. The same spa as 45 Park Lane next door.

The verdict

London’s classic grand hotel, made bright again, with the best dining of any hotel on my list.

Knightsbridge, on Hyde Park · Visited November 2026

The Emory

All-suite modern landmark (Maybourne, Fora Reserve)

All 60 rooms are suites, starting at 50 square meters, which is huge for London. The building is by Richard Rogers (the Pompidou Centre, the Lloyd’s Building), suspended on its structure so you never feel the Piccadilly Line running underneath, and it won a 2026 RIBA award. Six designers each did two floors, all on a warm wood-grain theme, so the rooms vary in tone and style.

Right for
Privacy-seekers and high-profile guests, wellness travelers, anyone who wants space in London
Room I’d book
The Corner Park Suite.The standout room, big enough for a proper dining table. A junior suite and a suite can also be connected and closed off as a private apartment.
Booking with me
A junior suite with a guaranteed park-side upgrade.The park view is the point of this hotel, and booked elsewhere that upgrade is rare.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Everything is included. The full minibar is complimentary, alcohol included, plus a bottle of wine or champagne each day, chosen from a list through your butler.

A butler on WhatsApp. Multilingual, reachable by message, and your preferences and dietary needs are remembered for next time. In-room dining was curated in part by Rosie Huntington-Whiteley.

Four floors of wellness. A Tracy Anderson studio, a members’ club with health-focused food and drink, and two resident doctors three days a week (one was a GP to Queen Elizabeth II for 30 years) offering full health checks and longevity advice.

Skip the courtyard. Courtyard rooms are junior suites only, and I wouldn’t recommend them. The exception is the terrace level, where every junior suite has a private terrace, quieter and with private road access, and you can smoke outside.

New Year’s Eve. Free-flowing champagne for in-house guests, with views of the London Eye fireworks.

Return stays. Found a floor and a design you love? Ask for it again next time.

Fora Reserve. The Emory is part of Fora Reserve, so my clients receive Fora perks on eligible stays, like daily breakfast and a hotel credit, at the same rate as booking direct.

The verdict

All suites, everything included, and wellness that goes far beyond a hotel spa.

Wilton Place, Knightsbridge · Visited November 2026

The Berkeley

Contemporary Knightsbridge classic (Maybourne, Fora Reserve)

The Berkeley has more buzz than its sister hotels, and it plays the contemporary one next to Claridge’s and The Connaught. It’s bigger than it looks from the entrance (190 rooms and suites, in a C-shaped building), every textile was recently refreshed, and the room designs vary across the hotel.

Right for
Travelers who want energy and style in Knightsbridge, with Emory-level wellness next door
Room I’d book
A first-floor balcony or terrace room (pictured).Consistent in design: warm wood and felt walls, somewhere between a Japanese listening bar and a 1960s or ’70s café.

Keith’s notes

Fora Reserve. The Berkeley is part of Fora Reserve, so my clients receive Fora perks on eligible stays, like daily breakfast and a hotel credit, at the same rate as booking direct.

The Emory’s wellness, included. Berkeley guests can use the four-floor wellness facility at The Emory next door.

The Berkeley Bar & Terrace. Curved bouclé sofas under a sculpted plaster frieze. A beautiful spot for a drink.

Christmas on the roof. The rooftop pool turns into a seasonal pop-up over the holidays.

The verdict

The livelier choice in Knightsbridge, with The Emory’s wellness on tap.

Grosvenor Square, Mayfair · Visited November 2026

The Chancery Rosewood

Grand contemporary landmark (Rosewood)

Grand, and the design is genuinely beautiful, with striking artwork throughout and the polish you expect from Rosewood. It’s the most international-feeling hotel on my London list: sleek and contemporary, the kind of hotel that would feel just as at home in New York.

Right for
Return visitors to London, design and art lovers, travelers who want a sleek, contemporary base in Mayfair

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

The private residence. The one I toured feels like a Mayfair apartment: a living room over Grosvenor Square, dining for six, a wood-paneled study, a walk-in dressing room, marble bathrooms and a full kitchen.

Service to the Rosewood standard. No doubt about it, and I fully expect guests have incredible stays here.

Come for the design. Beautiful interiors and artwork throughout. It’s contemporary first, rather than distinctly British.

First time in London? For classic, unmistakably British London, I’d start you at its sister, Rosewood London in Holborn, and save The Chancery for a return trip.

The verdict

Beautiful, grand and international in feel. My pick for return visitors who want contemporary design over old-London tradition.

Hyde Park Corner, Knightsbridge · Visited November 2026

The Lanesborough

Regency palace (Oetker Collection)

Quintessentially British: a Grade I listed Regency building on Hyde Park Corner with 93 rooms, half of them suites, and around 360 staff. Every room category comes with a personal butler, and the service has the natural, family warmth of its Oetker Collection sisters, Le Bristol and Hotel du Cap.

Right for
Travelers who want classic British London, multi-generational families, service lovers
Room I’d book
The Royal Suite, for the whole family.Up to seven bedrooms across an entire side of the building, about 400 square meters. For couples, ask about the new top-floor Pearl rooms with balconies.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

A butler in every room. Not just the suites. Every category includes one.

Service is the difference. London’s best rooms are all excellent now. Service is what sets the top hotels apart, and it’s where The Lanesborough shines.

Café LPM and afternoon tea. The first café from La Petite Maison, plus a signature tea and an official Bridgerton partnership.

A real spa and club. A subterranean spa, around seven classes a day, and club membership for your stay.

The verdict

Classic British London with service that stands apart, and suites big enough for the whole family.

High Holborn · Visited November 2026

Rosewood London

Classic grand hotel (Rosewood)

This felt like classic, old luxury London: a grand Edwardian building with more than 300 rooms, and the rooms and suites are large for the city. It’s a great base for families and business travelers, and the easiest recommendation I can make for a first trip to London.

Right for
First-time London visitors, families, business travelers
Room I’d book
Ask about the one-bedroom suite.It’s massive, and the standard rooms are generous too.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Eat and drink in. A beautiful bistro-style restaurant, a bar on the World’s 50 Best list, and a lovely high tea.

Romance, with a caveat. Maybe not the best fit for younger honeymooners or a romantic getaway. For couples 45 to 50 and up, the classic feel works well.

The verdict

My pick for a first trip to London, and for families.

Sloane Street, Chelsea · Visited November 2026

The Cadogan, A Belmond Hotel

Intimate townhouse (Belmond)

A small, polished hotel in a lovely, quiet corner of Chelsea. It feels residential rather than touristy, with a lot of London’s best shopping right outside the door. For someone who has already done the sights and wants to live like a Londoner for a few days, this is the one.

Right for
Return visitors to London, couples, shoppers, travelers who want quiet
Room I’d book
The Deluxe Junior Suite on the fifth floor.My favorite room in the hotel, with a cool, unique layout. Note there are three stairs inside, so tell me if anyone in your party has mobility concerns.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Only 54 rooms. And only seven of those are entry and superior rooms, so it books up quickly. Ask early.

Quiet, not sleepy. A residential feel, but Sloane Street and Knightsbridge shopping are steps away.

The verdict

My favorite for return visitors: intimate, quiet and beautifully located.

Hyde Park Corner · Visited November 2026

The Peninsula London

Modern palace (Peninsula)

The location is the first selling point: overlooking the park at Hyde Park Corner. Then the dining, which is some of the most fun in London, from the venue built around old rally cars to a beautiful Michelin-starred restaurant. The design is unmistakably Peninsula, polished and precise.

Right for
Ultra-VIPs, privacy seekers, large families and groups, food lovers
Room I’d book
The penthouses.Some of the largest penthouses I’ve ever seen, maybe in all of London, with private entrances.

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Very private. Private entrances and the kind of discretion that makes it my first call for high-profile guests.

Book dinner in. Between the rally-car venue and the Michelin-starred restaurant, plan at least one night on property.

The verdict

My pick for ultra-VIPs and anyone who wants space and privacy.

Broadwick Street, Soho · Visited November 2026

Broadwick Soho

Maximalist boutique

Unique, maximalist and young, right in the heart of Soho. There’s a lot of story behind this place, and the art is a big part of it, with Warhol pieces and much more on the walls. If you want to walk out the door into the action, this is your hotel.

Right for
Art lovers, younger travelers, anyone who wants to be in the middle of it

Read the full review

Keith’s notes

Come for the art. Warhol pieces and more throughout. Ask me for the backstory before you go.

This is Soho. Lively day and night. Choose it for the energy, not for quiet.

The verdict

The most fun address on my London list, for art lovers who want the action.
